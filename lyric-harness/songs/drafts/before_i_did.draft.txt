Grey coat, wool, on the rail at the back of the shop
Tray of buttons by the till, a bell, and the clock
Deep in the pocket, two of them, an aisle
Neat, the tag said; her dress in the old style
Held in one hand, turned over, and read
Two names, and a date, and a scrap of veil
A face I know, half turned round, looking at me
Straight, my mother stood, as she told the tale
End of the war, the year they were wed
No, not a wind of it in my family
Somebody loved this coat before I did
And wore it warm through a winter of Sundays
Somebody loved this coat before I did
And left them folded in the pocket to find
I wear it now. Somebody chose this coat
The date is right. I know the hand that wrote
