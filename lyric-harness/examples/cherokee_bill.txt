He was Crawford Goldsby: gold in the name and cold in the hand,
and the Territory told him young exactly where to stand;
they cut him down to Cherokee Bill the summer he learned to kill,
and a short name travels faster than a long one ever will.
He rode with Cook, and Cook rode wide from Wagoner to Wann,
a Winchester across the horn, a boy inside the man;
they took the Katy at the bend, they took the store at noon,
and the posse ate their dust and swore they'd have him by the moon.
At Lenapah he sent a round through a window and a wall,
and Ernest Melton, hanging paper, never heard it call;
one round, one room, one man who came to hang a strip of trim,
and every rope in Arkansas began to braid for him.
It wasn't law that took him down, it was a friend's own floor:
Ike Rogers watched him bend to light a smoke, and nothing more
than one split stick of stove wood dropped the boy none could outrun.
He went down in a kitchen. He went down without a gun.
They walked him into Fort Smith where the basement never dried,
where the hell-upon-the-border kept its sentenced men beside
the ones still waiting trial, and he got a pistol through,
and Lawrence Keating went down guarding a corridor he knew.
Judge Parker never raised his voice, and never had to shout;
he'd read the Territory's ledger and he'd worked the balance out:
a land that grows without a law will grow one out of rope,
and teach in one arithmetic what it could not teach in hope.
March seventeenth, eighteen ninety-six. They asked him for a word.
He said, I came here to die, not make a speech, and that was heard
from Fort Smith to the Nation, and it's traveling there still,
and a short name travels faster than a long one ever will.
