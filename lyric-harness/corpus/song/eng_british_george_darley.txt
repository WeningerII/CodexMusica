--- TITLE: MAY DAY.
--- SOURCE: PG26715
[VERSE 1]
O may, thou art a merry time,
  Sing hi! the hawthorn pink and pale!
When hedge-pipes they begin to chime,
  And summer-flowers to sow the dale.
[VERSE 2]
When lasses and their lovers meet
  Beneath the early village-thorn,
And to the sound of tabor sweet
  Bid welcome to the Maying-morn!
[VERSE 3]
O May, thou art a merry time,
  Sing hi! the hawthorn pink and pale!
When hedge-pipes they begin to chime,
  And summer-flowers to sow the dale.
[VERSE 4]
When grey-beards and their gossips come
  With crutch in hand our sports to see,
And both go tottering, tattling home,
  Topful of wine as well as glee!
[VERSE 5]
O May, thou art a merry time,
  Sing hi! the hawthorn pink and pale!
When hedge-pipes they begin to chime,
  And summer-flowers to sow the dale.
[VERSE 6]
But Youth was aye the time for bliss,
  So taste it, Shepherds! while ye may:
For who can tell that joy like this
  Will come another holiday?
[VERSE 7]
O May, thou art a merry time,
  Sing hi! the hawthorn pink and pale!
When hedge-pipes they begin to chime,
  And summer-flowers to sow the dale.

--- TITLE: I'VE BEEN ROAMING.
--- SOURCE: PG26715
[VERSE 1]
I 've been roaming! I 've been roaming!
  Where the meadow dew is sweet,
And like a queen I 'm coming
  With its pearls upon my feet.
[VERSE 2]
I 've been roaming! I 've been roaming!
  O'er red rose and lily fair,
And like a sylph I 'm coming
  With their blossoms in my hair.
[VERSE 3]
I 've been roaming! I 've been roaming!
  Where the honeysuckle creeps,
And like a bee I 'm coming
  With its kisses on my lips.
[VERSE 4]
I 've been roaming! I 've been roaming!
  Over hill and over plain,
And like a bird I 'm coming
  To my bower back again!

--- TITLE: SYLVIA'S SONG.
--- SOURCE: PG26715
[VERSE 1]
The streams that wind amid the hills
  And lost in pleasure slowly roam,
While their deep joy the valley fills,--
  Even these will leave their mountain home;
    So may it, Love! with others be,
    But I will never wend from thee.
[VERSE 2]
The leaf forsakes the parent spray,
  The blossom quits the stem as fast;
The rose-enamour'd bird will stray
  And leave his eglantine at last:
    So may it, Love! with others be,
    But I will never wend from thee.

--- TITLE: SERENADE.
--- SOURCE: PG26715
[VERSE 1]
Awake thee, my Lady-love!
  Wake thee, and rise!
The sun through the bower peeps
  Into thine eyes!
[VERSE 2]
Behold how the early lark
  Springs from the corn!
Hark, hark how the flower-bird
  Winds her wee horn!
[VERSE 3]
The swallow's glad shriek is heard
  All through the air!
The stock-dove is murmuring
  Loud as she dare!
[VERSE 4]
Apollo's winged bugleman
  Cannot contain,
But peals his loud trumpet-call
  Once and again!
[VERSE 5]
Then wake thee, my Lady-love,
  Bird of my bower!
The sweetest and sleepiest
  Bird at this hour!

--- TITLE: Song
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
Sweet in her green dell the flower of beauty slumbers,
  Lull’d by the faint breezes sighing through her hair;
Sleeps she and hears not the melancholy numbers
  Breathed to my sad lute ’mid the lonely air.
[VERSE 2]
Down from the high cliffs the rivulet is teeming
  To wind round the willow banks that lure him from above:
O that in tears, from my rocky prison streaming,
  I too could glide to the bower of my love!
[VERSE 3]
Ah! where the woodbines with sleepy arms have wound her,
  Opes she her eyelids at the dream of my lay,
Listening, like the dove, while the fountains echo round her,
  To her lost mate’s call in the forests far away.
[VERSE 4]
Come then, my bird! For the peace thou ever bearest,
  Still Heaven’s messenger of comfort to me--
Come--this fond bosom, O faithfullest and fairest,
  Bleeds with its death-wound, its wound of love for thee!

--- TITLE: To Helene
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
I sent a ring--a little band
  Of emerald and ruby stone,
And bade it, sparkling on thy hand,
  Tell thee sweet tales of one
    Whose constant memory
    Was full of loveliness, and thee.
[VERSE 2]
A shell was graven on its gold,--
  ’Twas Cupid fix’d without his wings--
To Helene once it would have told
  More than was ever told by rings:
    But now all’s past and gone,
    Her love is buried with that stone.
[VERSE 3]
Thou shalt not see the tears that start
  From eyes by thoughts like these beguiled;
Thou shalt not know the beating heart,
  Ever a victim and a child:
    Yet Helene, love, believe
    The heart that never could deceive.
[VERSE 4]
I’ll hear thy voice of melody
  In the sweet whispers of the air;
I’ll see the brightness of thine eye
  In the blue evening’s dewy star;
    In crystal streams thy purity;
    And look on Heaven to look on thee.

--- TITLE: The Fallen Star
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
A star is gone! a star is gone!
  There is a blank in Heaven;
One of the cherub choir has done
  His airy course this even.
[VERSE 2]
He sat upon the orb of fire
  That hung for ages there,
And lent his music to the choir
  That haunts the nightly air.
[VERSE 3]
But when his thousand years are pass’d,
  With a cherubic sigh
He vanish’d with his car at last,
  For even cherubs die!
[VERSE 4]
Hear how his angel-brothers mourn--
  The minstrels of the spheres--
Each chiming sadly in his turn
  And dropping splendid tears.
[VERSE 5]
The planetary sisters all
  Join in the fatal song,
And weep this hapless brother’s fall,
  Who sang with them so long.
[VERSE 6]
But deepest of the choral band
  The Lunar Spirit sings,
And with a bass-according hand
  Sweeps all her sullen strings.
[VERSE 7]
From the deep chambers of the dome
  Where sleepless Uriel lies,
His rude harmonic thunders come
  Mingled with mighty sighs.
[VERSE 8]
The thousand car-borne cherubim,
  The wandering eleven,
All join to chant the dirge of him
  Who fell just now from Heaven.

--- TITLE: "IT IS NOT BEAUTY I DEMAND"
--- SOURCE: PG2620
[VERSE 1]
It is not Beauty I demand,
A crystal brow, the moon's despair,
Nor the snow's daughter, a white hand,
Nor mermaid's yellow pride of hair:
[VERSE 2]
Tell me not of your starry eyes,
Your lips that seem on roses fed,
Your breasts, where Cupid tumbling lies
Nor sleeps for kissing of his bed: -
[VERSE 3]
A bloomy pair of vermeil cheeks
Like Hebe's in her ruddiest hours,
A breath that softer music speaks
Than summer winds a-wooing flowers, -
[VERSE 4]
These are but gauds: nay, what are lips?
Coral beneath the ocean-stream,
Whose brink when your adventurer sips
Full oft he perisheth on them.
[VERSE 5]
And what are cheeks but ensigns oft
That wave hot youth to fields of blood?
Did Helen's breast, though ne'er so soft,
Do Greece or Ilium any good?
[VERSE 6]
Eyes can with baleful ardor burn;
Poison can breathe, that erst perfumed;
There's many a white hand holds an urn
With lovers' hearts to dust consumed.
[VERSE 7]
For crystal brows - there's naught within;
They are but empty cells for pride;
He who the Siren's hair would win
Is mostly strangled in the tide.
[VERSE 8]
Give me, instead of Beauty's bust,
A tender heart, a loyal mind
Which with temptation I could trust,
Yet never linked with error find, -
[VERSE 9]
One in whose gentle bosom I
Could pour my secret heart of woes,
Like the care-burthened honey-fly
That hides his murmurs in the rose, -
[VERSE 10]
My earthly Comforter! whose love
So indefeasible might be
That, when my spirit won above,
Hers could not stay, for sympathy.

--- TITLE: THE FLOWER OF BEAUTY
--- SOURCE: PG2620
[VERSE 1]
Sweet in her green dell the flower of beauty slumbers,
Lulled by the faint breezes sighing through her hair;
Sleeps she, and hears not the melancholy numbers
Breathed to my sad lute amid the lonely air?
[VERSE 2]
Down from the high cliffs the rivulet is teeming
To wind round the willow-banks that lure him from above:
Oh that, in tears from my rocky prison streaming,
I too could glide to the bower of my love!
[VERSE 3]
Ah, where the woodbines with sleepy arms have wound her,
Opes she her eyelids at the dream of my lay,
Listening like the dove, while the fountains echo round her,
To her lost mate's call in the forest far away?
[VERSE 4]
Come, then, my bird! for the peace thou ever bearest,
Still Heaven's messenger of comfort be to me;
Come! this fond bosom, my faithfulest, my fairest,
Bleeds with its death-wound, - but deeper yet for thee.

--- TITLE: LAST NIGHT
--- SOURCE: PG2620
[VERSE 1]
I sat with one I love last night,
She sang to me an olden strain;
In former times it woke delight,
Last night - but pain.
[VERSE 2]
Last night we saw the stars arise,
But clouds soon dimmed the ether blue:
And when we sought each other's eyes
Tears dimmed them too!
[VERSE 3]
We paced along our favorite walk,
But paced in silence broken-hearted:
Of old we used to smile and talk;
Last night - we parted.
