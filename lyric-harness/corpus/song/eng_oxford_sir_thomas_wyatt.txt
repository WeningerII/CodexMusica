--- TITLE: Forget not yet
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
Forget not yet the tried intent
Of such a truth as I have meant;
My great travail so gladly spent,
Forget not yet!
[VERSE 2]
Forget not yet when first began
The weary life ye know, since whan
The suit, the service, none tell can;
Forget not yet!
[VERSE 3]
Forget not yet the great assays,
The cruel wrong, the scornful ways,
The painful patience in delays,
Forget not yet!
[VERSE 4]
Forget not! O, forget not this!--
How long ago hath been, and is,
The mind that never meant amiss--
Forget not yet!
[VERSE 5]
Forget not then thine own approved,
The which so long hath thee so loved,
Whose steadfast faith yet never moved:
Forget not this!

--- TITLE: The Appeal
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
And wilt thou leave me thus!
Say nay, say nay, for shame!
--To save thee from the blame
Of all my grief and grame.
And wilt thou leave me thus?
    Say nay! say nay!
[VERSE 2]
And wilt thou leave me thus,
That hath loved thee so long
In wealth and woe among:
And is thy heart so strong
As for to leave me thus?
    Say nay! say nay!
[VERSE 3]
And wilt thou leave me thus,
That hath given thee my heart
Never for to depart
Neither for pain nor smart:
And wilt thou leave me thus?
    Say nay! say nay!
[VERSE 4]
And wilt thou leave me thus,
And have no more pitye
Of him that loveth thee?
Alas, thy cruelty!
And wilt thou leave me thus?
    Say nay! say nay!

--- TITLE: A Revocation
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
What should I say?
--Since Faith is dead.
And Truth away
  From you is fled?
  Should I be led
    With doubleness?
    Nay! nay! mistress.
[VERSE 2]
I promised you,
  And you promised me,
To be as true
  As I would be.
  But since I see
    Your double heart,
    Farewell my part!
[VERSE 3]
Thought for to take
  ’Tis not my mind;
But to forsake
  One so unkind;
  And as I find
    So will I trust.
    Farewell, unjust!
[VERSE 4]
Can ye say nay
  But that you said
That I alway
  Should be obeyed?
  And--thus betrayed
    Or that I wist!
    Farewell, unkist!

--- TITLE: Vixi Puellis Nuper Idoneus ...
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
They flee from me that sometime did me seek,
  With naked foot stalking within my chamber:
Once have I seen them gentle, tame, and meek,
  That now are wild, and do not once remember
  That sometime they have put themselves in danger
To take bread at my hand; and now they range,
Busily seeking in continual change.
[VERSE 2]
Thanked be fortune, it hath been otherwise
  Twenty times better; but once especial--
In thin array: after a pleasant guise,
  When her loose gown did from her shoulders fall,
  And she me caught in her arms long and small,
And therewithal so sweetly did me kiss,
And softly said, ‘_Dear heart, how like you this?_’
[VERSE 3]
It was no dream; for I lay broad awaking:
  But all is turn’d now, through my gentleness,
Into a bitter fashion of forsaking;
  And I have leave to go of her goodness;
  And she also to use new-fangleness.
But since that I unkindly so am servèd,
‘_How like you this?_’--what hath she now deservèd?

--- TITLE: To His Lute
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
My lute, awake! perform the last
Labour that thou and I shall waste,
  And end that I have now begun;
For when this song is said and past,
  My lute, be still, for I have done.
[VERSE 2]
As to be heard where ear is none,
As lead to grave in marble stone,
  My song may pierce her heart as soon:
Should we then sing, or sigh, or moan?
  No, no, my lute! for I have done.
[VERSE 3]
The rocks do not so cruelly
Repulse the waves continually,
  As she my suit and affectiòn;
So that I am past remedy:
  Whereby my lute and I have done.
[VERSE 4]
Proud of the spoil that thou hast got
Of simple hearts thorough Love’s shot,
  By whom, unkind, thou hast them won;
Think not he hath his bow forgot,
  Although my lute and I have done.
[VERSE 5]
Vengeance shall fall on thy disdain,
That makest but game of earnest pain:
  Trow not alone under the sun
Unquit to cause thy lover’s plain,
  Although my lute and I have done.
[VERSE 6]
May chance thee lie wither’d and old
The winter nights that are so cold,
  Plaining in vain unto the moon:
Thy wishes then dare not be told:
  Care then who list! for I have done.
[VERSE 7]
And then may chance thee to repent
The time that thou has lost and spent
  To cause thy lover’s sigh and swoon:
Then shalt thou know beauty but lent,
  And wish and want as I have done.
[VERSE 8]
Now cease, my lute! this is the last
Labour that thou and I shall waste,
  And ended is that we begun:
Now is this song both sung and past--
  My lute, be still, for I have done.
