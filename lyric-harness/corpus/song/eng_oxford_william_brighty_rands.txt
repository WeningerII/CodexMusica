--- TITLE: The Flowers
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
When Love arose in heart and deed
  To wake the world to greater joy,
‘What can she give me now?’ said Greed,
  Who thought to win some costly toy.
[VERSE 2]
He rose, he ran, he stoop’d, he clutch’d;
  And soon the Flowers, that Love let fall,
In Greed’s hot grasp were fray’d and smutch’d,
  And Greed said, ‘Flowers! Can this be all?’
[VERSE 3]
He flung them down and went his way,
  He cared no jot for thyme or rose;
But boys and girls came out to play,
  And some took these and some took those--
[VERSE 4]
Red, blue, and white, and green and gold;
  And at their touch the dew return’d,
And all the bloom a thousandfold--
  So red, so ripe, the roses burn’d!

--- TITLE: The Thought
--- SOURCE: PG66619
[VERSE 1]
Into the skies, one summer’s day,
I sent a little Thought away;
Up to where, in the blue round,
The sun sat shining without sound.
[VERSE 2]
Then my Thought came back to me.--
Little Thought, what did you see
In the regions whence you come?
And when I spoke, my Thought was dumb.
[VERSE 3]
But she breathed of what was there,
In the pure bright upper air;
And, because my Thought so shone,
I knew she had been shone upon.
[VERSE 4]
Next, by night a Thought I sent
Up into the firmament;
When the eager stars were out,
And the still moon shone about.
[VERSE 5]
And my Thought went past the moon
In between the stars, but soon
Held her breath and durst not stir,
For the fear that covered her;
Then she thought, in this demur:
[VERSE 6]
‘Dare I look beneath the shade,
Into where the worlds are made;
Where the suns and stars are wrought?
Shall I meet another Thought?
[VERSE 7]
‘Will that other Thought have wings?
Shall I meet strange, heavenly things?
Thought of Thoughts, and Light of Lights,
Breath of Breaths, and Night of Nights?’
[VERSE 8]
Then my Thought began to hark
In the illuminated dark,
Till the silence, over, under,
Made her heart beat more than thunder.
[VERSE 9]
And my Thought, came trembling back,
But with something on her track,
And with something at her side;
Nor till she has lived and died,
Lived and died, and lived again,
Will that awful thing seem plain.

--- TITLE: The First Tooth
--- SOURCE: PG2619
[VERSE 1]
There once was a wood, and a very thick wood,
So thick that to walk was as much as you could;
But a sunbeam got in, and the trees understood.
[VERSE 2]
I went to this wood, at the end of the snows,
And as I was walking I saw a primrose;
Only one!  Shall I show you the place where it grows?
[VERSE 3]
There once was a house, and a very dark house,
As dark, I believe, as the hole of a mouse,
Or a tree in my wood, at the thick of the boughs.
[VERSE 4]
I went to this house, and I searched it aright,
I opened the chambers, and I found a light;
Only one!  Shall I show you this little lamp bright?
[VERSE 5]
There once was a cave, and this very dark cave
One day took a gift from an incoming wave;
And I made up my mind to know what the sea gave.
[VERSE 6]
I took a lit torch, I walked round the ness
When the water was lowest; and in a recess
In my cave was a jewel.  Will nobody guess?
[VERSE 7]
O there was a baby, he sat on my knee,
With a pearl in his mouth that was precious to me,
His little dark mouth like my cave of the sea!
[VERSE 8]
I said to my heart, "And my jewel is bright!
He blooms like a primrose!  He shines like a light!"
Put your hand in his mouth!  Do you feel?  He can bite!

--- TITLE: The Reformation of Godfrey Gore
--- SOURCE: PG2619
[VERSE 1]
Godfrey Gordon Gustavus Gore--
No doubt you have heard the name before--
Was a boy who never would shut a door!
[VERSE 2]
The wind might whistle, the wind might roar,
And teeth be aching and throats be sore,
But still he never would shut the door.
[VERSE 3]
His father would beg, his mother implore,
"Godfrey Gordon Gustavus Gore,
We really do wish you would shut the door!"
[VERSE 4]
Their hands they wrung, their hair they tore;
But Godfrey Gordon Gustavus Gore
Was deaf as the buoy out at the Nore.
[VERSE 5]
When he walked forth the folks would roar,
"Godfrey Gordon Gustavus Gore,
Why don't you think to shut the door?"
[VERSE 6]
They rigged out a Shutter with sail and oar,
And threatened to pack off Gustavus Gore
On a voyage of penance to Singapore.
[VERSE 7]
But he begged for mercy, and said, "No more!
Pray do not send me to Singapore
On a Shutter, and then I will shut the door!"
[VERSE 8]
"You will?" said his parents; "then keep on shore!
But mind you do!  For the plague is sore
Of a fellow that never will shut the door,
Godfrey Gordon Gustavus Gore!"

--- TITLE: The Wonderful World
--- SOURCE: PG2619
[VERSE 1]
Great, wide, beautiful, wonderful World,
With the wonderful water round you curled,
And the wonderful grass upon your breast,
World, you are beautifully dressed.
[VERSE 2]
The wonderful air is over me,
And the wonderful wind is shaking the tree--
It walks on the water, and whirls the mills,
And talks to itself on the tops of the hills.
[VERSE 3]
You friendly Earth, how far do you go,
With the wheat-fields that nod and the rivers that flow,
With cities and gardens, and cliffs and isles,
And people upon you for thousands of miles?
[VERSE 4]
Ah! you are so great, and I am so small,
I tremble to think of you, World, at all;
And yet, when I said my prayers to-day,
A whisper inside me seemed to say,
"You are more than the Earth, though you are such a dot:
You can love and think, and the Earth cannot!"

--- TITLE: The Peddler's Caravan
--- SOURCE: PG2619
[VERSE 1]
I wish I lived in a caravan,
With a horse to drive, like a peddler-man!
Where he comes from nobody knows,
Or where he goes to, but on he goes!
[VERSE 2]
His caravan has windows two,
And a chimney of tin, that the smoke comes through;
He has a wife, with a baby brown,
And they go riding from town to town.
[VERSE 3]
Chairs to mend, and delf to sell!
He clashes the basins like a bell;
Tea-trays, baskets ranged in order,
Plates, with alphabets round the border!
[VERSE 4]
The roads are brown, and the sea is green,
But his house is like a bathing-machine;
The world is round, and he can ride,
Rumble and slash, to the other side!
[VERSE 5]
With the peddler-man I should like to roam,
And write a book when I came home;
All the people would read my book,
Just like the Travels of Captain Cook!
