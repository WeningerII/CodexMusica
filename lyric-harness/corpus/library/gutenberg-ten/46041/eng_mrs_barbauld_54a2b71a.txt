--- TITLE: How blest the sacred tie that binds [item: PG46041-479]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c479
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
How blest the sacred tie that binds,
In sweet communion, kindred minds!
How swift the heavenly course they run,
Whose hearts, whose faith, whose hopes are one!

[VERSE 2]
To each the soul of each how dear!
What tender love, what holy fear!
How doth the generous flame within
Refine from earth, and cleanse from sin!

[VERSE 3]
Their streaming eyes together flow
For human guilt and mortal woe;
Their ardent prayers together rise
Like mingling flames in sacrifice.

[VERSE 4]
Nor shall the glowing flame expire,
When dimly burns frail nature’s fire;
Then shall they meet in realms above,
A heaven of joy, a heaven of love.

--- TITLE: Again the Lord of light and life [item: PG46041-694]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c694
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
Again the Lord of light and life
Awakes the kindling ray,
Unseals the eyelids of the morn,
And pours increasing day.

[VERSE 2]
O what a night was that which wrapt
The heathen world in gloom!
O what a Sun which rose this day
Triumphant from the tomb!

[VERSE 3]
This day be grateful homage paid
And loud hosannas sung;
Let gladness dwell in every heart,
And praise on every tongue.

[VERSE 4]
Ten thousand different lips shall join
To hail this welcome morn,
Which scatters blessings from its wings
To nations yet unborn.

--- TITLE: Awake, my soul! lift up thine eyes [item: PG46041-847]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c847
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
Awake, my soul! lift up thine eyes;
See where thy foes against thee rise,
In long array, a numerous host;
Awake, my soul, or thou art lost.

[VERSE 2]
See where rebellious passions rage,
And fierce desires and lust engage;
The meanest foe of all the train
Has thousands and ten thousands slain.

[VERSE 3]
Thou treadest upon enchanted ground;
Perils and snares beset thee round;
Beware of all; guard every part;
But most, the traitor in thy heart.

[VERSE 4]
Come, then, my soul! now learn to wield
The weight of thine immortal shield;
Put on the armor from above,
Of heavenly truth, and heavenly love.

--- TITLE: Blest is the man whose softening heart [item: PG46041-953]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c953
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
Blest is the man whose softening heart
Feels all another’s pain;
To whom the supplicating eye
Was never raised in vain;

[VERSE 2]
Whose breast expands with generous warmth
A stranger’s woes to feel;
And bleeds in pity o’er the wound
He wants the power to heal.

[VERSE 3]
He spreads his kind supporting arms,
To every child of grief;
His secret bounty largely flows,
And brings unasked relief.

[VERSE 4]
To gentle offices of love,
His feet are never slow;
He views, through mercy’s melting eye,
A brother in a foe.

[VERSE 5]
Peace from the bosom of his God,
The Saviour’s grace shall give;
And when he kneels before the throne,
His trembling soul shall live.

--- TITLE: How blest the righteous when he dies [item: PG46041-1039]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1039
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
How blest the righteous when he dies!
When sinks a weary soul to rest!
How mildly beam the closing eyes!
How gently heaves the expiring breast!

[VERSE 2]
So fades a summer cloud away;
So sinks the gale when storms are o’er;
So gently shuts the eye of day;
So dies a wave along the shore.

[VERSE 3]
A holy quiet reigns around,
A calm which life nor death destroys;
And nought disturbs that peace profound
Which his unfettered soul enjoys.

[VERSE 4]
Life’s labor done, as sinks the clay,
Light from its load the spirit flies,
While heaven and earth combine to say,
“How blest the righteous when he dies!”

--- TITLE: Not for the pious dead we weep [item: PG46041-1059]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1059
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Barbauld.

[VERSE 1]
Not for the pious dead we weep;
Their sorrows now are o’er;
The sea is calm, the tempest past,
On that eternal shore.

[VERSE 2]
Their peace is sealed, their rest is sure,
Within that better home:
Awhile we weep and linger here,
Then follow to the tomb.
