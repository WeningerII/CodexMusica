--- TITLE: Yes, for me, for me he careth [item: PG46041-99]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c99
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
Yes, for me, for me he careth
With a brother’s tender care;
Yes, with me, with me he shareth
Every burden, every fear.

[VERSE 2]
Yes, o’er me, o’er me he watcheth,
Ceaseless watcheth, night and day;
Yes, e’en me, e’en me he snatcheth
From the perils of the way.

[VERSE 3]
Yes, for me he standeth pleading
At the mercy-seat above;
Ever for me interceding,
Constant in untiring love.

[VERSE 4]
Yes, in me abroad he sheddeth
Joys unearthly, love and light;
And to cover me he spreadeth
His paternal wing of might.

[VERSE 5]
Yes, in me, in me he dwelleth;
I in him, and he in me!
And my empty soul he filleth,
Here and through eternity.

[VERSE 6]
Thus I wait for his returning,
Singing all the way to heaven:
Such the joyful song of morning
Such the tranquil song of even.

--- TITLE: O love of God, how strong and true [item: PG46041-110]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c110
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
O love of God, how strong and true!
Eternal and yet ever new:
Above all price, and still unbought;
Beyond all knowledge and all thought.

[VERSE 2]
O, wide-embracing, wondrous love,
We read thee in the sky above;
We read thee in the earth below,
In seas that swell and streams that flow.

[VERSE 3]
We read thee best in him who came
To bear for us the cross of shame;
Sent by the Father from on high,
Our life to live, our death to die.

[VERSE 4]
O love of God, our shield and stay
Through all the perils of the way;
Eternal love, in thee we rest,
For ever safe, for ever blest.

Left for us his glad abode;
Stooping from his throne of bliss,
To this darksome wilderness.

[VERSE 2]

Come to bid our sorrows cease;
Come to scatter, with his light,
All the shadows of our night.

Making the poor earth his home
Come to bear sin’s heavy load;
Son of David, Son of God.

Left for us his glad abode;
Son of Mary, Son of God!

Bringing with him from above,
Holy peace and holy love.

--- TITLE: A pilgrim through this lonely world [item: PG46041-150]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c150
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
A pilgrim through this lonely world,
The blessed Saviour passed;
A mourner all his life was he,
A dying Lamb at last.

[VERSE 2]
That tender heart which felt for all,
For us its life-blood gave;
It found on earth no resting-place,
Save only in the grave!

[VERSE 3]
Such was our Lord: and shall we fear
The cross with all its scorn?
Or love a faithless, evil world,
That wreathed his brow with thorn?

[VERSE 4]
No; facing all its frowns or smiles,
Like him, obedient still,
We homeward press, through storm or calm,
To Zion’s blessed hill.

--- TITLE: Lord, let thy Spirit penetrate [item: PG46041-417]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c417
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
Lord, let thy Spirit penetrate
This heart and soul of mine;
And my whole being with thy grace
Pervade, O Life divine!

[VERSE 2]
As this clear air surrounds the earth,
Thy grace around me roll;
As the fresh light pervades the air,
So pierce and fill my soul.

[VERSE 3]
As from these clouds drops down in love
The precious summer rain,
So from thyself pour down the flood
That freshens all again.

[VERSE 4]
As these fair flowers exhale their scent
In gladness at our feet,
So from thyself let fragrance breathe,
More heavenly and more sweet.

[VERSE 5]
Thus life within our lifeless hearts,
Shall make its glad abode;
And we shall shine in beauteous light
Filled with the light of God.

--- TITLE: Far down the ages now [item: PG46041-459]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c459
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
Far down the ages now,
Much of her journey done,
The pilgrim church pursues her way,
Until her crown be won.

[VERSE 2]
The story of the past
Comes up before her view:
How well it seems to suit her still—
Old, and yet ever new!

[VERSE 3]
It is the oft-told tale
Of sin and weariness—
Of grace and love yet flowing down
To pardon and to bless.

[VERSE 4]
No wider is the gate,
No broader is the way,
No smoother is the ancient path,
That leads to life and day.

[VERSE 5]
No sweeter is the cup,
Nor less our lot of ill:
’Twas tribulation ages since,
’Tis tribulation still.

[VERSE 6]
No slacker grows the fight,
No feebler is the foe,
Nor less the need of armor tried,
Of shield, and spear, and bow.

[VERSE 7]
Thus onward still we press,
Through evil and through good—
Through pain, and poverty, and want,
Through peril and through blood.

[VERSE 8]
Still faithful to our God,
And to our Captain true,
We follow where he leads the way,
The kingdom in our view.

--- TITLE: Silent, like men in solemn haste [item: PG46041-848]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c848
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
Silent, like men in solemn haste,
Girded wayfarers of the waste,
We press along the narrow road
That leads to life, to bliss, to God.

[VERSE 2]
We fling aside the weight and sin,
Resolved the victory to win;
We know the peril, but our eyes
Rest on the splendor of the prize.

[VERSE 3]
No idling now, no wasteful sleep;
We trim our lamps, our vigils keep;
No shrinking from the desperate fight,
No thought of yielding or of flight;

[VERSE 4]
No love of present gain nor ease,
No seeking man nor self to please.—
With the brave heart and steady eye,
We onward march to victory.

[VERSE 5]
Night is far spent, and morn is near—
Morn of the cloudless and the clear;
’Tis but a little and we come
To our reward, our crown, our home.

[VERSE 6]
Another year—it may be less—
And we have crossed the wilderness,
Finished the toil, the rest begun,
The battle fought, the triumph won.

--- TITLE: Thy way, not mine, O Lord [item: PG46041-913]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c913
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]
Thy way, not mine, O Lord!
However dark it be;
O lead me by thine own right hand;
Choose out the path for me.

[VERSE 2]
Smooth let it be, or rough,
It will be still the best;
Winding or straight, it matters not,
It leads me to thy rest.

[VERSE 3]
I dare not choose my lot,
I would not if I might;
But choose thou for me, O my God!
So shall I walk aright.

[VERSE 4]
The kingdom that I seek
Is thine; so let the way
That leads to it, O Lord! be thine,
Else I must surely stray.

[VERSE 5]
My portion thou! my cup
With joy or sorrow fill;
As ever best to thee may seem,
Choose thou my good and ill.

[VERSE 6]
Choose thou for me my friends,
My sickness or my health;
Choose thou my joys and cares for me,
My poverty or wealth.

[VERSE 7]
Not mine, not mine the choice,
In things or great or small;
Be thou my Guide, my Guard, my Strength,
My Wisdom, and my All.

Against thy power I strove.

Let not thy light depart;

Dissolve this iron heart!

[VERSE 3]

More pliable and mild;
In glad simplicity
More like a trustful child.

[VERSE 4]

And more, my God, of thee;

However rough it be.

More of thyself within.

--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c930
--- AUTHOR: Bonar.

[VERSE 1]

Making life’s desert places bloom and smile;
O for that faith to grasp the glad For ever,
Amid the shadows of earth’s Little While!

[VERSE 2]
A little while for patient vigil keeping,
To face the storm, to wrestle with the strong;
A little while to sow the seed with weeping,
Then bind the sheaves and sing the harvest-song.

[VERSE 3]
A little while to wear the vail of sadness,
To toil with weary step through miry ways,
Then to pour forth the fragrant oil of gladness,
And clasp the girdle round the robe of Praise!

[VERSE 4]
A little while, ’mid shadow and illusion,
To strive by faith love’s mysteries to spell,
Then read each dark enigma’s bright solution,
Then hail sight’s verdict—He doth all things well.

[VERSE 5]
And he who is himself the Gift and Giver,
The future glory and the present smile,
With the bright promise of the glad For ever,
Will light the shadows of earth’s Little While.

Her absent Lord to see;

She weeps a mourner yet.

Thine own fair world again.
Come, then, Lord Jesus, come!
