--- TITLE: As on the cross the Saviour hung [item: PG46041-176]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c176
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
As on the cross the Saviour hung,
And groaned, and bled, and died,
He looked with pity on a wretch
That languished by his side.

[VERSE 2]
The dying thief in Jesus saw
A majesty divine;
While scoffing Jews around him stood,
And asked him for a sign!

[VERSE 3]
The kingdom, Lord, is thine, he said;
’Tis thine o’er men to reign:
Thy wondrous works thy lordship prove,
These pains thy love proclaim:

[VERSE 4]
Honors divine await thee soon,
A scepter and a crown:
With shame thy foes shall yet behold
Thee seated on a throne.

[VERSE 5]
Then, gracious Lord, remember me!
Is not forgiveness thine?
My crimes have brought me to thy side—
Thy love brought thee to mine!

[VERSE 6]
His prayer the dying Jesus hears,
And instantly replies,
To-day your parting soul shall be
With me in paradise.

--- TITLE: The great Redeemer we adore [item: PG46041-378]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c378
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
The great Redeemer we adore,
Who came the lost to seek and save—
Went humbly down from Jordan’s shore
To find a tomb beneath its wave!

[VERSE 2]
With thee into thy watery tomb,
Lord, ’tis our glory to descend;
’Tis wondrous grace that gives us room
To share the grave of such a friend.

[VERSE 3]
Yet, as the yielding waves give way
To let us see the light again,
So, on the resurrection day,
The bands of death proved weak and vain.

[VERSE 4]
Thus, when thou shalt again appear,
The gates of death shall open wide:
Our dust thy mighty voice shall hear,
And rise and triumph at thy side.

--- TITLE: See how the willing converts trace [item: PG46041-379]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c379
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
See how the willing converts trace
The path their great Redeemer trod:
And follow through his liquid grave
The meek, the lowly Son of God!

[VERSE 2]
Here they renounce their former deeds,
And to a heavenly life aspire,
Their rags for glorious robes exchanged,
They shine in clean and bright attire.

[VERSE 3]
O sacred rite, by thee the name
Of Jesus we to own begin;
This is our resurrection pledge,
Pledge of the pardon of our sin.

[VERSE 4]
Glory to God on high be given,
Who shows his grace to sinful men;
Let saints on earth, and hosts in heaven,
In concert join their loud Amen.

--- TITLE: Lord, at thy table we behold [item: PG46041-523]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c523
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
Lord, at thy table we behold
The wonders of thy grace;
But most of all admire that we
Should find a welcome place.

[VERSE 2]
What strange, surprising grace is this,
That we, so lost, have room!
Jesus our weary souls invites,
And freely bids us come!

[VERSE 3]
Ye saints below, and hosts of heaven,
Join all your sacred powers:
No theme is like redeeming love;
No Saviour is like ours.

--- TITLE: Another six days’ work is done [item: PG46041-616]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c616
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
Another six days’ work is done;
Another day of rest begun,
Return, my soul, enjoy the rest,
Improve the day thy God hath blest.

[VERSE 2]
O that our thoughts and thanks may rise,
As grateful incense to the skies;
And draw from heaven that sweet repose
Which none but he that feels it knows.

[VERSE 3]
This heavenly calm within the breast
Is the dear pledge of glorious rest,
Which for the Church of God remains,
The end of cares, the end of pains.

--- TITLE: How various and how new [item: PG46041-652]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c652
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
How various and how new
Are thy compassions, Lord!
Each morning shall thy mercies show,
Each night thy truth record.

[VERSE 2]
Thy goodness, like the sun,
Dawned on our early days,
Ere infant reason had begun
To form our lips to praise.

[VERSE 3]
Each object we beheld
Gave pleasure to our eyes;
And nature all our senses held
In bands of sweet surprise.

[VERSE 4]
But pleasures more refined
Awaited that blest day,
When light arose upon our mind
And chased our sins away.

[VERSE 5]
How new thy mercies, then!
How sovereign and how free!
Our souls, that had been dead in sin,
Were made alive to thee.

--- TITLE: Come, every pious heart [item: PG46041-670]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c670
--- AUTHOR: Stennett.

[VERSE 1]
Come, every pious heart
That loves the Saviour’s name,
Your noblest powers exert
To celebrate his fame:
Tell all above and all below
The debt of love to him you owe.

[VERSE 2]
Such was his zeal for God,
And such his love for you,
He nobly undertook
What angels could not do;
His every deed of love and grace
All words exceed, all thoughts surpass.

[VERSE 3]
He left his starry crown,
And laid his robes aside;
On wings of love came down,
And wept, and bled, and died;
What he endured, O who can tell,
To save our souls from death and hell!

[VERSE 4]
From the dark grave he rose,
The mansion of the dead;
And thence his mighty foes
In glorious triumph led;
Up through the sky the Conqueror rode,
And reigns on high the Son of God.

[VERSE 5]
From thence he’ll quickly come,
His chariot will not stay,
And bear our spirits home
To realms of endless day:
There shall we see his lovely face,
And ever be in his embrace.
