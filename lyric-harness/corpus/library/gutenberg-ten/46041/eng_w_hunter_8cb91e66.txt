--- TITLE: You may sing of the beauty of mountain and dale [item: PG46041-463]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c463
--- AUTHOR: W. Hunter.

[VERSE 1]
You may sing of the beauty of mountain and dale,
Of the silvery streamlets and flowers of the vale;
But the place most delightful this earth can afford,
Is the place of devotion, the house of the Lord.

[VERSE 2]
You may boast of the sweetness of day’s early dawn,
Of the sky’s softening graces when day is just gone;
But there’s no other season or time can compare,
With the hour of devotion, the season of prayer.

[VERSE 3]
You may value the friendships of youth and of age,
And select for your comrades the noble and sage;
But the friends that most cheer me on life’s rugged road,
Are the friends of my Master, the children of God.

[VERSE 4]
You may talk of your prospects, of fame, or of wealth,
And the hopes that oft flatter the favorites of health;
But the hope of bright glory, of heavenly bliss—
Take away every other, and give me but this.

[VERSE 5]
Ever hail, blessed temple, abode of my Lord!
I will turn to thee often, to hear from his word;
I will walk to thine altar with those that I love,
And rejoice in the prospects revealed from above.

--- TITLE: Away from his home and the friends of his youth [item: PG46041-1095]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1095
--- AUTHOR: W. Hunter.

[VERSE 1]
Away from his home and the friends of his youth,
He hasted, the herald of mercy and truth,
For the love of his Lord, and to seek for the lost:
Soon, alas! was his fall—but he died at his post.

[VERSE 2]
The stranger’s eye wept, that, in life’s brightest bloom,
One gifted so highly should sink to the tomb;
For in ardor he led in the van of the host,
And he fell like a soldier—he died at his post.

[VERSE 3]
He wept not himself that his warfare was done—
The battle was fought, and the victory won;
But he whispered of those whom his heart clung to most,
“Tell my brethren, for me, that I died at my post.”

[VERSE 4]
He asked not a stone to be sculptured with verse;
He asked not that fame should his merits rehearse;
But he asked as a boon, when he gave up the ghost,
That his brethren might know that he died at his post.

[VERSE 5]
Victorious his fall—for he rose as he fell,
With Jesus, his Master, in glory to dwell:
He has passed o’er the stream, and has reached the bright coast,
For he fell like a martyr—he died at his post.

[VERSE 6]
And can we the words of his exit forget?
O! no; they are fresh in our memory yet:
An example so worthy shall never be lost,
We will fall in the work—we will die at our post.

--- TITLE: There is a place where my hopes are stayed [item: PG46041-1159]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1159
--- AUTHOR: W. Hunter.

[VERSE 1]
There is a place where my hopes are stayed,
My heart and my treasure are there;
Where verdure and blossoms never fade,
And fields are eternally fair.

[CHORUS]
That blissful place is my father-land;
By faith its delights I explore;
Come, favor my flight, angelic band,
And waft me in peace to the shore.

[VERSE 2]
There is a place where the angels dwell,
A pure and peaceful abode;
The joys of that place no tongue can tell;
For there is the palace of God!

[VERSE 3]
There is a place where my friends are gone
Who suffered and worshiped with me!
Exalted with Christ, high on his throne,
The King in his beauty they see.

[VERSE 4]
There is a place where I hope to live
When life and its labors are o’er,
A place which the Lord to me will give,
And then I shall sorrow no more.
