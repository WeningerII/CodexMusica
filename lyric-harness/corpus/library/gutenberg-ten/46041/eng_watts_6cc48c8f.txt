--- TITLE: There is a stream whose gentle flow [item: PG46041-4]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c4
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
There is a stream whose gentle flow
Supplies the city of our God;
Life, love, and joy, still gliding through,
And watering our divine abode.

[VERSE 2]
That sacred stream, thy holy word,
Supports our faith, our fear controls;
Sweet peace thy promises afford,
And give new strength to fainting souls.

[VERSE 1]
Jehovah reigns: he dwells in light,
Arrayed with majesty and might;
The world, created by his hands,
Still on its firm foundation stands.

Himself the ever-living God.

And everlasting holiness

--- TITLE: Greatest of beings, source of life [item: PG46041-46]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c46
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Greatest of beings, source of life;
Sovereign of air, and earth, and sea!
All nature feels thy pow’r, and all
A silent homage pay to thee.

[VERSE 2]
Waked by thy hand, the morning sun
Pours forth to thee its earlier rays,
And spreads thy glories as it climbs;
While raptured worlds look up and praise.

[VERSE 3]
The moon, to the deep shades of night,
Speaks the mild luster of thy name;
While all the stars, that cheer the scene,
Thee, the great Lord of light, proclaim.

[VERSE 4]
And groves and vales, and rocks and hills,
And every flower, and every tree,
Ten thousand creatures, warm with life,
Have each a grateful song for thee.

[VERSE 5]
But man was formed to rise to heaven;
And, blest with reason’s clearer light,
He views his Maker through his works,
And glows with rapture at the sight.

[VERSE 6]
Nor can the thousand songs that rise,
Whether from air, or earth, or sea,
So well repeat Jehovah’s praise,
Or raise such sacred harmony.

--- TITLE: I’ll praise my Maker while I’ve breath [item: PG46041-72]

--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
I’ll praise my Maker while I’ve breath,
And when my voice is lost in death,
Praise shall employ my nobler powers:
My days of praise shall ne’er be past,
While life and thought and being last,
And immortality endures.

[VERSE 2]
Happy the man whose hopes rely
On Israel’s God: he made the sky,
And earth, and seas, with all their train.
His truth for ever stands secure:
He saves th’ oppressed, he feeds the poor,
And none shall find his promise vain.

[VERSE 3]
The Lord pours eyesight on the blind;
The Lord supports the fainting mind,
He sends the laboring conscience peace:
He helps the stranger in distress,
The widow and the fatherless,
And grants the prisoner sweet release.

[VERSE 4]
I’ll praise him while he gives me breath,
And when my voice is lost in death,
Praise shall employ my nobler powers:
My days of praise shall ne’er be past,
While life, and thought, and being last,
And immortality endures.

--- TITLE: Let the whole race of creatures lie [item: PG46041-89]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c89
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Let the whole race of creatures lie
In dust before the Lord!
Whate’er his powerful hand has formed,
He governs with a word.

[VERSE 2]
Ten thousand ages ere the skies
Were into motion brought,
All the long years and worlds to come
Stood present to his thought.

[VERSE 3]
There’s not a sparrow, or a worm,
O’erlooked in his decrees:
He raises monarchs to a throne,
Or sinks with equal ease.

[VERSE 4]
If light attend the course I go,
’Tis he provides the rays;
And ’tis his hand that hides the sun,
If darkness cloud my days.

[VERSE 5]
Trusting his wisdom and his love,
I would not wish to know
What, in the book of his decrees,
Awaits me here below.

[VERSE 6]
Be this alone my fervent prayer:
Whate’er my lot may be,
Or joys, or sorrows—may they form
My soul for heaven and thee!

--- TITLE: Father! how wide thy glory shines [item: PG46041-111]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c111
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Father! how wide thy glory shines!
How high thy wonders rise!
Known through the earth by thousand signs,
By thousand through the skies.

[VERSE 2]
Those mighty orbs proclaim thy power,
Their motions speak thy skill;
And on the wings of every hour,
We read thy patience still.

[VERSE 3]
But when we view thy strange design
To save rebellious worms,
Where justice and compassion join
In their divinest forms,

[VERSE 4]
Our thoughts are lost in reverent awe,
We love and we adore;
The brightest angel never saw
So much of God before.

[VERSE 5]
Here the whole Deity is known;
But thought can never trace
Which of the glories brighter shine,
The justice, or the grace.

[VERSE 6]
Now the full glories of the Lamb
Adorn the heavenly plains:
Bright seraphs learn Immanuel’s name,
And try their choicest strains.

[VERSE 7]
O! may I bear some humble part
In that immortal song;
Wonder and joy shall tune my heart,
And love command my tongue.

--- TITLE: He dies, the friend of winners dies [item: PG46041-172]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c172
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
He dies, the friend of winners dies!
Lo! Salem’s daughters weep around!
A solemn darkness vails the skies,
A sudden trembling shakes the ground.

[VERSE 2]
Here’s love and grief beyond degree!
The Lord of glory dies for men!
But, lo! what sudden joys we see—
Jesus the dead revives again!

[VERSE 3]
The rising Lord forsakes the tomb!
(The tomb in vain forbids his rise!)
Cherubic legions guard him home,
And shout him welcome to the skies!

[VERSE 4]
Break off your tears, you saints, and tell
How high our great Deliverer reigns;
Sing how he spoiled the hosts of hell,
And led the monster Death in chains.

[VERSE 5]
Say, “Live for ever, wondrous King!
Born to redeem, and strong to save!”
Then ask the monster, “Where’s thy sting?
And where’s thy victory, boasting grave?”

Blessed be the everlasting God,
The Father of our Lord;
Be his abounding mercy praised,
His majesty adored.

[VERSE 2]
When from the dead he raised his Son,
And called him to the sky,
He gave our souls a lively hope
That they should never die.

[VERSE 3]
What though the first man’s sin requires
Our flesh to see the dust;
Yet, as the Lord our Saviour rose,
So all his followers must.

[VERSE 4]
There’s an inheritance divine,
Reserved against that day;
’Tis uncorrupted, undefiled,
And can not fade away!

[VERSE 5]
Saints by the power of God are kept,
Till the salvation come;
We walk by faith as strangers here,
Till Christ shall take us home.

--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Faith adds new charms to earthly bliss,
And saves us from its snares;
It yields support in all our toils,
And softens all our cares.

[VERSE 2]
The wounded conscience knows its power
The healing balm to give;
That balm the saddest heart can cheer,
And make the dying live.

[VERSE 3]
Unvailing wide the heavenly world,
Where endless pleasures reign,
It bids us seek our portion there,
Nor bids us seek in vain.

[VERSE 4]
There, still unshaken, would we rest
Till this frail body dies;
And then, on faith’s triumphant wing,
To endless glory rise.

--- TITLE: Mistaken souls, that dream of heaven [item: PG46041-354]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c354
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Mistaken souls, that dream of heaven,
And make their empty boast
Of inward joys, and sins forgiven,
While they are slaves to lust!

[VERSE 2]
How vain are fancy’s airy flights,
If faith be cold and dead!
None but a living power unites
To Christ, the living Head.

[VERSE 3]
’Tis faith that purifies the heart;
’Tis faith that works by love;
That bids all sinful joys depart,
And lifts the thoughts above.

[VERSE 4]
Faith must obey our Father’s will,
As well as trust his grace;
A pardoning God requires us still
To walk in all his ways.

[VERSE 5]
This faith shall every fear control
By its celestial power,
With holy triumph fill the soul
In death’s approaching hour.

--- TITLE: Death can not make our souls afraid [item: PG46041-1054]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1054
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
Death can not make our souls afraid,
If God be with us there;
We may walk through its darkest shade,
And never yield to fear.

[VERSE 2]
I could renounce my all below,
If my Redeemer bid;
And run, if I were called to go,
And die as Moses did.

[VERSE 3]
Might I but climb to Pisgah’s top,
And view the promised land,
My flesh itself would long to drop,
And welcome the command.

[VERSE 4]
Clasped in my heavenly Father’s arms,
I would forget my breath,
And lose my life among the charms
Of so divine a death.

--- TITLE: The day is past and gone [item: PG46041-1197]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1197
--- AUTHOR: Watts.

[VERSE 1]
The day is past and gone,
The evening shades appear;
O may we all remember well
The night of death draws near.

[VERSE 2]
We lay our garments by,
Upon our beds to rest;
So death will soon disrobe us all
Of what we now possess.

[VERSE 3]
Lord, keep us safe this night,
Secure from every fear,
Beneath the pinions of thy love,
Till morning light appear.

[VERSE 4]
And when we early rise,
To view the unwearied sun,
May we set out to win the prize
And after glory run.

[VERSE 5]
And when our days are past,
And we from time remove,
O may we in thy bosom rest—
The bosom of thy love.
