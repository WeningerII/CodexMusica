--- TITLE: Upon the gospel’s sacred page [item: PG46041-6]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c6
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
Upon the gospel’s sacred page
The gathered beams of ages shine;
And, as it hastens, every age
But makes its brightness more divine.

[VERSE 2]
On mightier wing, in loftier flight,
From year to year, does knowledge soar;
And, as it soars, the gospel light
Becomes effulgent more and more.

[VERSE 3]
More glorious still, as centuries roll,
New regions blest, new powers unfurled;
Expanding with the expanding soul,
Its radiance shall o’erflow the world;

[VERSE 4]
Flow to restore, but not destroy;
As when the cloudless lamp of day
Pours out its flood of light and joy,
And sweeps the lingering mist away.

--- TITLE: The heavenly spheres to thee, O God [item: PG46041-54]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c54
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
The heavenly spheres to thee, O God,
Attune their evening hymn;
All-wise, all-holy, thou art praised
In song of seraphim.
Unnumbered systems, suns, and worlds,
Unite to worship thee,
While thy majestic greatness fills
Space, time, eternity.

[VERSE 2]
Nature, a temple worthy thee,
Beams with thy light and love;
Whose flowers so sweetly bloom below,
Whose stars rejoice above;
Whose altars are the mountain cliffs
That rise along the shore;
Whose anthems, the sublime accord
Of storm and ocean roar.

[VERSE 3]
Her song of gratitude is sung
By spring’s awakening hours;
Her summer offers at thy shrine
Its earliest, loveliest flowers;
Her autumn brings its golden fruits,
In glorious luxury given;
While winter’s silver hights reflect
Thy brightness back to heaven.

--- TITLE: God is love; his mercy brightens [item: PG46041-116]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c116
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
God is love; his mercy brightens
All the path in which we move!
Bliss he grants, and woe he lightens;
God is light, and God is love.

[VERSE 2]
Chance and change are busy ever;
Worlds decay and ages move;
But his mercy waneth never;
God is light, and God is love.

[VERSE 3]
E’en the hour that darkest seemeth,
His unchanging goodness proves;
From the cloud his brightness streameth;
God is light, and God is love.

[VERSE 4]
He our earthly cares entwineth
With his comforts from above;
Everywhere his glory shineth;
God is light, and God is love.

--- TITLE: Watchman, tell us of the night [item: PG46041-128]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c128
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
Watchman, tell us of the night,
What its signs of promise are.
Traveler, o’er yon mountain’s hights
See that glory-beaming star!

[VERSE 2]
Watchman, does its beauteous ray
Aught of joy or hope foretell?
Traveler, yes: it brings the day,
Promised day of Israel.

[VERSE 3]
Watchman, tell us of the night;
Higher yet that star ascends.
Traveler, blessedness and light,
Peace and truth, its course portends.

[VERSE 4]
Watchman, will its beams alone
Gild the spot that gave them birth?
Traveler, ages are its own:
See! it bursts o’er all the earth!

[VERSE 5]
Watchman, tell us of the night,
For the morning seems to dawn.
Traveler, darkness takes its flight,
Doubt and terror are withdrawn.

[VERSE 6]
Watchman, let thy wandering cease;
Hie thee to thy quiet home.
Traveler, lo! the Prince of Peace,
Lo! the Son of God is come!

--- TITLE: How sweetly flowed the gospel sound [item: PG46041-141]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c141
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
How sweetly flowed the gospel sound
From lips of gentleness and grace,
When listening thousands gathered round,
And joy and gladness filled the place!

[VERSE 2]
From heaven he came, of heaven he spoke,
To heaven he led his followers’ way;
Dark clouds of gloomy night he broke,
Unvailing an immortal day.

[VERSE 3]
“Come, wanderers, to my Father’s home:
Come, all ye weary ones, and rest!”
Yes, sacred Teacher, we will come,
Obey thee, love thee, and be blest.

--- TITLE: From the recesses of a lowly spirit [item: PG46041-588]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c588
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
From the recesses of a lowly spirit,
Our humble prayer ascends; O Father! hear it,
Upsoaring on the wings of awe and meekness;
Forgive its weakness!

[VERSE 2]
We see thy hand: it leads us, it supports us;
We hear thy voice: it counsels and it courts us:
And then we turn away; and still thy kindness
Forgives our blindness.

[VERSE 3]
O, how long-suffering, Lord! but thou delightest
To win with love the wandering; thou invitest,
By smiles of mercy, not by frowns or terrors,
Man from his errors.

[VERSE 4]
Father and Saviour! plant within each bosom
The seeds of holiness, and bid them blossom
In fragrance and in beauty bright and vernal,
And spring eternal.

--- TITLE: Father of spirits! humbly bent before thee [item: PG46041-671]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c671
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
Father of spirits! humbly bent before thee,
Songs of glad homage unto thee we bring:
Touched by thy Spirit, O teach us to adore thee;
Let thy light attend us,
Let thy love befriend us,
Father of our spirits, Everlasting King!

[VERSE 2]
Send forth thy mandate, gather in the nations,
Through the wide universe thy name be known,
Millions of voices shall join in adorations,
Every soul invited,
Every voice united;
Joining to adore thee, Everlasting One!

--- TITLE: The offerings to thy throne which rise [item: PG46041-695]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c695
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
The offerings to thy throne which rise,
Of mingled praise and prayer,
Are but a worthless sacrifice
Unless the heart is there.

[VERSE 2]
Upon thine all-discerning ear
Let no vain words intrude;
No tribute but the vow sincere—
The tribute of the good.

[VERSE 3]
My offerings will indeed be blest,
If sanctified by thee—
If thy pure Spirit touch my breast
With its own purity.

[VERSE 4]
O, may that Spirit warm my heart
To piety and love,
And to life’s lowly vale impart
Some rays from heaven above.

--- TITLE: O let my trembling soul be still [item: PG46041-902]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c902
--- AUTHOR: Bowring.

[VERSE 1]
O let my trembling soul be still,
While darkness vails this mortal eye,
And wait thy wise, thy holy will,
Wrapped yet in fears and mystery;
I can not, Lord, thy purpose see;
Yet all is well, since ruled by thee.

[VERSE 2]
So trusting in thy love, I tread
The narrow path of duty on;
What though some cherished joys are fled?
What though some flattering dreams are gone?
Yet purer, nobler joys remain,
And peace is won through conquered pain.
