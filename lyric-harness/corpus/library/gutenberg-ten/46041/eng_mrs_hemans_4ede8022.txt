--- TITLE: He knelt; the Saviour knelt and prayed [item: PG46041-165]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c165
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Hemans.

[VERSE 1]
He knelt; the Saviour knelt and prayed,
When but his Father’s eye
Looked, through the lonely garden shade,
On that dread agony;
The Lord of high and heavenly birth
Was bowed with sorrow unto death.

[VERSE 2]
The sun went down in fearful hour;
The heavens might well grow dim,
When this mortality had power
Thus to o’ershadow him;
That he who came to save might know
The very depths of human woe.

[VERSE 3]
He knew them all—the doubt, the strife,
The faint, perplexing dread;
The mists that hang o’er parting life
All darkened round his head;
And the Deliverer knelt to pray;
Yet passed it not, that cup, away.

[VERSE 4]
It passed not, though the stormy wave
Had sunk beneath his tread;
It passed not, though to him the grave
Had yielded up its dead;
But there was sent him, from on high,
A gift of strength, for man to die.

[VERSE 5]
And was his mortal hour beset
With anguish and dismay?
How may we meet our conflict yet
In the dark, narrow way?
How, but through him that path who trod:
“Save, or we perish, Son of God.”

--- TITLE: O thou! who, in the olive shade [item: PG46041-1012]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1012
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Hemans.

[VERSE 1]
O thou! who, in the olive shade,
When the dark hour came on,
Didst, with a breath of heavenly aid,
Strengthen thy suffering Son;

[VERSE 2]
O, by the anguish of that night,
Send us now blest relief;
Or to the chastened, let thy might
Hallow this whelming grief.

[VERSE 3]
And thou, that, when the starry sky,
Saw the dread strife begun,
Didst teach adoring faith to cry,
Father! thy will be done;

[VERSE 4]
By thy meek Spirit, thou, of all
That e’er have mourned the chief,
Blest Saviour! if the stroke must fall,
Hallow this whelming grief.

--- TITLE: Lowly and solemn be [item: PG46041-1087]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1087
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Hemans.

[VERSE 1]
Lowly and solemn be
Thy children’s cry to thee,
Father divine;
A hymn of suppliant breath,
Owning that life and death
Alike are thine.

[VERSE 2]
O Father, in that hour,
When earthly help and power
Are all in vain,
When spears, and shield, and crown,
In faintness are cast down,
Do thou sustain.

[VERSE 3]
By him who bowed to take
The death-cup for our sake,
The thorn, the rod—
From whom the last dismay
Was not to pass away—
Aid us, O God.

[VERSE 4]
Trembling beside the grave,
We call on thee to save,
Father divine:
Hear, hear our suppliant breath;
Keep us, in life and death,
Thine, only thine.
