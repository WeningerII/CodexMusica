--- TITLE: Father of mercies! God of love [item: PG46041-81]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c81
--- AUTHOR: Raffles.

[VERSE 1]
Father of mercies! God of love!
My Father and my God!
I’ll sing the honors of thy name,
And spread thy praise abroad.

[VERSE 2]
In every period of my life
Thy thoughts of love appear;
Thy mercies gild each transient scene,
And crown each passing year.

[VERSE 3]
In all thy mercies, may my soul
A Father’s bounty see;
Nor let the gifts thy grace bestows
Estrange my heart from thee.

[VERSE 4]
Teach me, in times of deep distress,
To own thy hand, O God!
And in submissive silence learn
The lessons of thy rod.

[VERSE 5]
Then may I close my eyes in death,
Redeemed from anxious fear:
For death itself, my God, is life,
If thou be with me there.

--- TITLE: Thou art my hiding-place, O Lord [item: PG46041-563]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c563
--- AUTHOR: Raffles.

[VERSE 1]
Thou art my hiding-place, O Lord,
In thee I fix my trust,
Encouraged by thy holy word,
A feeble child of dust.

[VERSE 2]
I have no argument beside,
I urge no other plea,
And ’tis enough—the Saviour died,
The Saviour died for me.

[VERSE 3]
When storms of fierce temptation beat,
And furious foes assail,
My refuge is the mercy-seat,
My hope within the vail.

[VERSE 4]
From strife of tongues and bitter words,
My spirit flies to thee;
Joy to my heart the thought affords—
My Saviour died for me.

[VERSE 5]
And when thy awful voice commands
This body to decay,
And life, in its last lingering sands,
Is ebbing fast away—

[VERSE 6]
Then, though it be in accents weak,
My voice shall call on thee,
And ask for strength in death to speak—
“My Saviour died for me.”

--- TITLE: Blest hour, when mortal man retires [item: PG46041-679]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c679
--- AUTHOR: Raffles.

[VERSE 1]
Blest hour, when mortal man retires
To hold communion with his God,
To send to heaven his warm desires,
And listen to the sacred word.

[VERSE 2]
Blest hour, when earthly cares resign
Their empire o’er his anxious breast,
While, all around, the calm divine,
Proclaims the holy day of rest.

[VERSE 3]
Blest hour, when God himself draws nigh,
Well pleased his people’s voice to hear,
To hush the penitential sigh,
And wipe away the mourner’s tear.

[VERSE 4]
Blest hour! for, where the Lord resorts,
Foretastes of future bliss are given,
And mortals find his earthly courts
The house of God, the gate of heaven.

--- TITLE: High in yonder realms of light [item: PG46041-1138]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1138
--- AUTHOR: Raffles.

[VERSE 1]
High in yonder realms of light,
Dwell the raptured saints above;
Far beyond our feeble sight,
Happy in Immanuel’s love:
Once they knew, like us below,
Pilgrims in this vale of tears,
Torturing pain and heavy woe,
Gloomy doubts, distressing fears.

[VERSE 2]
’Mid the chorus of the skies,
’Mid the angelic lyres above,
Hark, their songs melodious rise,
Songs of praise to Jesus’ love!
Happy spirits, ye are fled
Where no grief can entrance find;
Lulled to rest the aching head,
Soothed the anguish of the mind.

[VERSE 3]
All is tranquil and serene,
Calm and undisturbed repose;
There no cloud can intervene,
There no angry tempest blows;
Every tear is wiped away,
Sighs no more shall heave the breast,
Night is lost in endless day,
Sorrow—in eternal rest.
