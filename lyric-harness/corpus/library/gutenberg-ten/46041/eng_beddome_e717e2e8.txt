--- TITLE: When Israel through the desert passed [item: PG46041-5]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c5
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
When Israel through the desert passed,
A fiery pillar went before,
To guide them through the dreary waste,
And lessen the fatigues they bore.

[VERSE 2]
Such is thy glorious word, O God;
’Tis for our light and guidance given;
It sheds a luster all abroad,
And points the path to bliss and heaven.

[VERSE 3]
It fills the soul with sweet delight,
And quickens its inactive powers;
It sets our wandering footsteps right,
Displays thy love and kindles ours.

[VERSE 4]
Its promises rejoice our hearts;
Its doctrine is divinely true;
Knowledge and pleasure it imparts;
It comforts and instructs us too.

[VERSE 5]
Ye favored lands, who have this word!
Ye saints, who feel its saving power!
Unite your tongues to praise the Lord,
And his distinguished grace adore.

--- TITLE: O Lord, thy perfect word [item: PG46041-18]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c18
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
O Lord, thy perfect word
Directs our steps aright;
Nor can all other books afford
Such profit or delight.

[VERSE 2]
Celestial light it sheds
To cheer this vale below;
To distant lands its glory spreads,
And streams of mercy flow.

[VERSE 3]
True wisdom it imparts;
Commands our hope and fear:
O may we hide it in our hearts,
And feel its influence there!

--- TITLE: Wait, O my soul, thy Maker’s will [item: PG46041-69]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c69
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Wait, O my soul, thy Maker’s will;
Tumultuous passions, all be still!
Nor let a murmuring thought arise;
His ways are just, his counsels wise.

[VERSE 2]
He in the thickest darkness dwells,
Performs his work, the cause conceals;
But, though his methods are unknown,
Judgment and truth support his throne.

[VERSE 3]
In heaven, and earth, and air, and seas,
He executes his firm decrees;
And by his saints it stands confest,
That what he does is ever best.

[VERSE 4]
Wait then, my soul, submissive wait,
Prostrate before his awful seat;
And, ’midst the terrors of his rod,
Trust in a wise and gracious God.

--- TITLE: Since God is mine, then present things [item: PG46041-88]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c88
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Since God is mine, then present things
And things to come are mine;
Yea, Christ, his word, and Spirit, too,
And glory all divine.

[VERSE 2]
Since he is mine, then from his love
He every trouble sends;
All things are working for my good,
And bliss his rod attends.

[VERSE 3]
Since he is mine, I need not fear
The rage of earth and hell;
He will support my feeble power,
Their utmost force repel.

[VERSE 4]
Since he is mine, let friends forsake,
Let wealth and honors flee:
Sure, he who giveth me himself,
Is more than these to me.

[VERSE 5]
Since he is mine, I’ll boldly pass
Through death’s dark, lonely vale:
He is my comfort and my stay,
When heart and flesh shall fail.

[VERSE 6]
And now, O Lord, since thou art mine,
What can I wish beside?
My soul shall at the fountain live,
When all the streams are dried.

--- TITLE: Did Christ o’er sinners weep [item: PG46041-161]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c161
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Did Christ o’er sinners weep,
And shall our cheeks be dry?
Let tears of penitential grief
Flow forth from every eye.

[VERSE 2]
The Son of God in tears,
The wondering angels see;
Be thou astonished, O my soul,
He shed those tears for thee.

[VERSE 3]
He wept that we might weep,
Each sin demands a tear,
In heaven alone no sin is found
And there’s no weeping there.

--- TITLE: God, in the gospel of his Son [item: PG46041-268]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c268
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
God, in the gospel of his Son,
Makes his eternal counsels known;
’Tis here his richest mercy shines,
And truth is drawn in fairest lines.

[VERSE 2]
Here sinners of a humble frame
May taste his grace and learn his name;
’Tis writ in characters of blood,
Severely just—immensely good.

[VERSE 3]
Here Jesus, in ten thousand ways,
His soul-attracting charms displays;
Recounts his poverty and pains,
And tells his love in melting strains.

[VERSE 4]
May this blest volume ever lie
Close to my heart, and near my eye—
Till life’s last hour my soul engage,
And be my chosen heritage!

--- TITLE: How free and boundless is the grace [item: PG46041-287]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c287
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
How free and boundless is the grace
Of our redeeming God!
Extending to the Greek and Jew,
And men of every blood.

[VERSE 2]
Come, all you wretched sinners, come,
He’ll form your souls anew;
His gospel and his heart have room
For rebels such as you.

[VERSE 3]
His doctrine is almighty love;
There’s virtue in his name
To turn a raven to a dove,
A lion to a lamb.

[VERSE 4]
Come, then, accept the offered grace,
And make no more delay;
His love will all your guilt efface,
And soothe your fears away.

--- TITLE: And must I part with all I have [item: PG46041-360]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c360
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
And must I part with all I have,
Jesus, my Lord! for thee?
This is my joy, since thou hast done
Much more than this for me.

[VERSE 2]
Yes, let it go; one look from thee
Will more than make amends
For all the losses I sustain
Of credit, riches, friends.

[VERSE 3]
Ten thousand worlds, ten thousand lives,
How worthless they appear,
Compared with thee, supremely good,
Divinely bright and fair.

[VERSE 4]
Saviour of souls! while I from thee
A single smile obtain,
Though destitute of all things else,
I’ll glory in my gain.

--- TITLE: Father of mercies, bow thine ear [item: PG46041-467]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c467
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Father of mercies, bow thine ear,
Attentive to our earnest prayer:
We plead for those who plead for thee;
Successful pleaders may they be.

[VERSE 2]
How great their work! how vast their charge!
Do thou their anxious souls enlarge:
Their best endowments are our gain;
We share the blessings they obtain.

[VERSE 3]
O, clothe with energy divine
Their words; and let those words be thine;
To them thy sacred truth reveal;
Suppress their fears, inflame their zeal.

[VERSE 4]
Teach them to sow the precious seed;
Teach them thy chosen flock to feed;
Teach them immortal souls to gain—
And thus reward their toil and pain.

[VERSE 5]
Let thronging multitudes around,
Hear from their lips the joyful sound,
In humble strains thy grace implore,
And feel thy Spirit’s living power.

--- TITLE: Ye men and angels, witness now [item: PG46041-489]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c489
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Ye men and angels, witness now—
Before the Lord we speak,
To him we make our solemn vow—
A vow we dare not break:

[VERSE 2]
That, long as life itself shall last,
Ourselves to Christ we yield;
Nor from his cause will we depart,
Or ever quit the field.

[VERSE 3]
We trust not in our native strength,
But on his grace rely;
May he, with our returning wants,
All needful aid supply.

[VERSE 4]
O, guide our doubtful feet aright,
And keep us in thy ways;
And, while we turn our vows to prayers,
Turn thou our prayers to praise.

--- TITLE: Let party names no more [item: PG46041-497]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c497
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Let party names no more
The Christian world o’erspread,
Gentile and Jew, and bond and free,
Are one in Christ, their Head.

[VERSE 2]
Among the saints on earth
Let mutual love be found;
Heirs of the same inheritance,
With mutual blessings crowned.

[VERSE 3]
Thus will the church below
Resemble that above,
Where streams of pleasure ever flow,
And every heart is love.

--- TITLE: Jesus, my love, my chief delight [item: PG46041-807]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c807
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Jesus, my love, my chief delight,
For thee I long, for thee I pray,
Amid the shadows of the night,
Amid the business of the day.

[VERSE 2]
When shall I see thy smiling face,
That face which I have often seen?
Arise, thou Sun of Righteousness!
Scatter the clouds that intervene.

[VERSE 3]
Thou art the glorious gift of God,
To sinners weary and distressed;
The first of all his gifts bestowed,
And certain pledge of all the rest.

[VERSE 4]
Since I can say this gift is mine,
I’ll tread the world beneath my feet,
No more at poverty repine,
Nor envy the rich sinner’s state.

--- TITLE: My times of sorrow and of joy [item: PG46041-1006]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1006
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
My times of sorrow and of joy,
Great God! are in thy hand;
My choicest comforts come from thee,
And go at thy command.

[VERSE 2]
If thou shouldst take them all away,
Yet would I not repine;
Before they were possessed by me,
They were entirely thine.

[VERSE 3]
Nor would I drop a murmuring word,
Though all the world were gone,
But seek enduring happiness
In thee, and thee alone.

--- TITLE: Behold the day is come [item: PG46041-1115]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1115
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
Behold the day is come;
The righteous Judge is near;
And sinners, trembling at their doom,
Shall soon their sentence hear.

[VERSE 2]
Angels, in bright attire,
Conduct him through the skies;
Darkness and tempest, smoke and fire,
Attend him as he flies.

[VERSE 3]
How awful is the sight!
How loud the thunders roar!
The sun forbears to give his light,
And stars are seen no more.

[VERSE 4]
The whole creation groans;
But saints arise and sing:
They are the ransomed of the Lord,
And he their God and King.

--- TITLE: My few revolving years [item: PG46041-1241]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1241
--- AUTHOR: Beddome.

[VERSE 1]
My few revolving years,
How swift they glide away!
How short the term of life appears,
When past—but as a day.

[VERSE 2]
Lord, through another year,
If thou permit my stay,
With watchful care may I pursue
The true and living way.
