--- TITLE: Beyond where Cedron’s waters flow [item: PG46041-164]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c164
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Beyond where Cedron’s waters flow,
Behold the suffering Saviour go
To sad Gethsemane;
His countenance is all divine,
Yet grief appears in every line.

[VERSE 2]
He bows beneath the sins of men;
He cries to God, and cries again,
In sad Gethsemane:
He lifts his mournful eyes above—
“My Father, can this cup remove?”

[VERSE 3]
With gentle resignation still,
He yielded to his Father’s will
In sad Gethsemane;
“Behold me here, thine only Son;
And, Father, let thy will be done.”

[VERSE 4]
The Father heard; and angels, there,
Sustained the Son of God in prayer,
In sad Gethsemane:
He drank the dreadful cup of pain—
Then rose to life and joy again.

[VERSE 5]
When storms of sorrow round us sweep,
And scenes of anguish make us weep,
To sad Gethsemane
We’ll look, and see the Saviour there,
And humbly bow, like him, in prayer.

--- TITLE: Meekly in Jordan’s flowing stream [item: PG46041-384]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c384
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Meekly in Jordan’s flowing stream
The great Redeemer bowed;
Bright was the glory’s sacred beam
That hushed the wondering crowd.

[VERSE 2]
Thus God descended to approve
The deed that Christ had done;
Thus came the emblematic Dove,
And hovered over the Son.

[VERSE 3]
So may the Spirit come to-day
To our baptismal scene;
Let thoughts of earth be far away,
And every mind serene.

[VERSE 4]
This day we give to holy joy;
This day to heaven belongs;
Raised to new life, we will employ
In melody our tongues.

--- TITLE: Planted in Christ, the living vine [item: PG46041-487]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c487
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Planted in Christ, the living vine,
This day, with one accord,
Ourselves, with humble faith and joy,
We yield to thee, O Lord.

[VERSE 2]
Joined in one body may we be;
One inward life partake;
One be our heart; one heavenly hope
In every bosom wake.

[VERSE 3]
In prayer, in effort, tears, and toils,
One wisdom be our guide;
Taught by one Spirit from above,
In thee may we abide.

[VERSE 4]
Around this feeble, trusting band,
Thy sheltering pinions spread,
Nor let the storms of trial beat
Too fiercely on our head.

[VERSE 5]
Then, when, among the saints in light,
Our joyful spirits shine,
Shall anthems of immortal praise,
O Lamb of God, be thine.

--- TITLE: The Prince of Salvation in triumph is riding [item: PG46041-606]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c606
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
The Prince of Salvation in triumph is riding,
And glory attends him along his bright way;
The news of his grace on the breezes is gliding,
And nations are owning his sway.

[VERSE 2]
And now thro’ the darkness of earth’s gloomy regions,
The wheels of his chariot are rolling sublime;
His banners unfolding his own true religion,
Dispelling the errors of time.

[VERSE 3]
Behold a bright angel from heaven descending,
High lifting his trumpet, hosannas to raise:
“Hail, Son of the Highest! let every knee bending,
Adore thee with offerings of praise.

[VERSE 4]
“Thy sword and thy buckler shall save and deliver
The poor and the needy, from foes that assail;
Thy bow and thy quiver shall vanquish for ever
The prince and the legions of hell.

[VERSE 5]
“Ride on in thy greatness, thou conquering Saviour;
Let thousands of thousands submit to thy reign,
Acknowledge thy goodness, entreat for thy favor,
And follow thy glorious train.

[VERSE 6]
“Ride on, till the compass of thy great dominion,
The globe shall encircle from pole unto pole;
And mankind, cemented with friendship and union,
Obey thee with heart and with soul.

[VERSE 7]
“Then loud shall ascend from each sanctified nation
The voice of thanksgiving, the chorus of praise;
And heaven shall echo the song of salvation,
In rich and melodious lays.”

--- TITLE: Sweet is the fading light of eve [item: PG46041-615]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c615
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Sweet is the fading light of eve;
And soft the sunbeams lingering there;
For these blest hours the world I leave,
Wafted on wings of praise and prayer.

[VERSE 2]
The time, how lovely and how still!
Peace shines and smiles on all below:
The plain, the stream, the wood, the hill,
All fair with evening’s setting glow.

[VERSE 3]
Season of rest! the tranquil soul
Feels the sweet calm, and melts to love,
And while these sacred moments roll,
Faith sees a smiling heaven above.

[VERSE 4]
Nor will our days of toil be long;
Our pilgrimage will soon be trod,
And we shall join the ceaseless song,
The endless sabbath of our God.

--- TITLE: Blest is the hour when cares depart [item: PG46041-712]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c712
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Blest is the hour when cares depart,
And earthly scenes are far—
When tears of woe forget to start,
And gently dawns upon the heart
Devotion’s holy star.

[VERSE 2]
Blest is the place where angels bend
To hear our worship rise,
Where kindred hearts their musings blend,
And all the soul’s affections tend
Beyond the vailing skies.

[VERSE 3]
Blest are the hallowed vows that bind
Man to his work of love—
Bind him to cheer the humble mind,
Console the weeping, lead the blind,
And guide to joys above.

[VERSE 4]
Sweet shall the song of glory swell,
Saviour divine, to thee,
When they whose work is finished well,
In thy own courts of rest shall dwell,
Blest through eternity.

--- TITLE: Sister, thou wast mild and lovely [item: PG46041-1076]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1076
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Sister, thou wast mild and lovely,
Gentle as the summer breeze,
Pleasant as the air of evening,
When it floats among the trees.

[VERSE 2]
Peaceful be thy silent slumber—
Peaceful in the grave so low:
Thou no more wilt join our number;
Thou no more our songs shalt know.

[VERSE 3]
Dearest sister, thou hast left us;
Here thy loss we deeply feel;
But ’tis God that hath bereft us:
He can all our sorrows heal.

[VERSE 4]
Yet again we hope to meet thee,
When the day of life is fled,
Then in heaven with joy to greet thee,
Where no farewell tear is shed.

--- TITLE: As flows the rapid river [item: PG46041-1088]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1088
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
As flows the rapid river,
With channel broad and free,
Its waters rippling ever,
And hastening to the sea;
So life is onward flowing,
And days of offered peace,
And man is swiftly going
Where calls of mercy cease.

[VERSE 2]
As moons are ever waning,
As hastes the sun away,
As stormy winds, complaining,
Bring on the wintery day:
So fast the night comes o’er us—
The darkness of the grave;
The death is just before us;
God takes the life he gave.

[VERSE 3]
Say, hath thy heart its treasure
Laid up in worlds above?
And is it all thy pleasure
Thy God to praise and love?
Beware lest death’s dark river
Its billows o’er thee roll,
And thou lament for ever
The ruin of thy soul.

--- TITLE: Yes, my native land, I love thee [item: PG46041-1281]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1281
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Yes, my native land, I love thee;
And all thy scenes, I love them well:
Home and friends, and happy country,
Can I bid you all farewell?
Can I leave you,
Far in heathen lands to dwell?

[VERSE 2]
Scenes of sacred peace and pleasure,
Holy days and Sabbath bell,
Richest, brightest, sweetest treasure,
Can I—can I say, farewell?
Can I leave you,
Far in heathen lands to dwell.

[VERSE 3]
Yes, I hasten from you gladly;
To the strangers let me tell
How he died—the blesséd Saviour—
To redeem a world from hell:
Let me hasten,
Far in heathen lands to dwell.

[VERSE 4]
Bear me on, thou restless ocean,
From the scenes I love so well:
Heaves my heart with warm emotion
While I go far hence to dwell:
Glad I bid thee,
Native land, farewell, farewell!

--- TITLE: Onward speed thy conquering flight [item: PG46041-1286]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1286
--- AUTHOR: S. F. Smith.

[VERSE 1]
Onward speed thy conquering flight,
Angel, onward speed!
Cast abroad thy radiant light,
Bid the shades recede;
Tread the idols in the dust,
Heathen fanes destroy;
Spread the gospel’s love and trust,
Spread the gospel’s joy.

[VERSE 2]
Onward speed thy conquering flight,
Angel, onward haste;
Quickly on each mountain hight
Be thy standard placed;
Let thy blissful tidings float
Far o’er vale and hill,
Till the sweetly-echoing note
Every bosom thrill.

[VERSE 3]
Onward speed thy conquering flight,
Angel, onward fly!
Long has been the reign of night;
Bring the morning nigh:
Unto thee earth’s sufferers lift
Their imploring wail;
Bear them heavén’s holy gift,
Ere their courage fail.

[VERSE 4]
Onward speed thy conquering flight,
Angel, onward speed!
Morning bursts upon our sight,
Lo! the time decreed:
Now the Lord his kingdom takes,
Thrones and empires fall;
Now the joyous song awakes,
“God is All in All!”
