--- TITLE: Lord, may the spirit of this feast [item: PG46041-519]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c519
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Sigourney.

[VERSE 1]
Lord, may the spirit of this feast—
The earnest of thy love—
Maintain a dwelling in our breast
Until we meet above.

[VERSE 2]
The healing sense of pardoned sin,
The hope that never tires,
The strength a pilgrim’s race to win,
The joy that heaven inspires:

[VERSE 3]
Still may their light our duties trace
In lines of hallowed flame,
Like that upon the prophet’s face,
When from the mount he came.

[VERSE 4]
But if no more with kindred dear
The broken bread we share,
Nor at the banquet board appear
To breathe the grateful prayer;

[VERSE 5]
Forget us not—when on the bed
Of dire disease we waste,
Or to the chambers of the dead,
And bar of judgment haste.

[VERSE 6]
Forget not—thou who bore the woe
Of Calvary’s fatal tree—
Those who within these courts below
Have thus remembered thee.

--- TITLE: When adverse winds and waves arise [item: PG46041-903]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c903
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Sigourney.

[VERSE 1]
When adverse winds and waves arise,
And in my heart despondence sighs;
When life her throng of cares reveals,
And weakness o’er my spirit steals,
Grateful I hear the kind decree,
That “as my day, my strength shall be.”

[VERSE 2]
When, with sad footsteps, memory roves
’Mid smitten joys and buried loves,
When sleep my tearful pillow flies,
And dewy morning drinks my sighs,
Still to thy promise, Lord! I flee,
That “as my day, my strength shall be.”

[VERSE 3]
One trial more must yet be past:
One pang—the keenest and the last;
And when, with brow convulsed and pale,
My feeble, quivering heart-strings fail,
Redeemer! grant my soul to see,
That “as my day, my strength shall be.”

--- TITLE: Onward, onward, men of heaven [item: PG46041-1275]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1275
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Sigourney.

[VERSE 1]
Onward, onward, men of heaven!
Bear the gospel banner high;
Rest not till its light is given—
Star of every pagan sky;
Send it where the pilgrim stranger
Faints beneath the torrid ray;
Bid the hearty forest ranger
Hail it ere he fades away.

[VERSE 2]
Where the Arctic ocean thunders,
Where the tropics fiercely glow,
Broadly spread its page of wonders,
Brightly bid its radiance flow;
India marks its luster stealing;
Shivering Greenland loves its rays,
Afric, ’mid her deserts kneeling,
Lifts the untaught strain of praise.

[VERSE 3]
Rude in speech, or wild in feature,
Dark in spirit, though they be,
Show that light to every creature—
Prince or vassal, bond or free:
Lo! they haste to every nation;
Host on host the ranks supply:
Onward! Christ is your salvation,
And your death is victory.

--- TITLE: Not for the summer hour alone [item: PG46041-1298]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1298
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Sigourney.

[VERSE 1]
Not for the summer hour alone,
When skies resplendent shine,
And youth and pleasure fill the throne,
Our hearts and hands we join.

[VERSE 2]
But for those stern and wintry days
Of sorrow, pain, and fear,
When heaven’s wise discipline doth make
Our earthly journey drear.

[VERSE 3]
Not for this span of life alone,
Which like a blast doth fly;
And, as the transient flowers of grass,
Just blossom, droop, and die.

[VERSE 4]
But for a being without end,
This vow of love we take;
Grant us, O Lord, one home at last,
For thy great mercy’s sake.
