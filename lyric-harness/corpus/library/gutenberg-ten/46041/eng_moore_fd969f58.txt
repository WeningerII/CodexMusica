--- TITLE: Thou art, O God, the life and light [item: PG46041-49]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c49
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
Thou art, O God, the life and light
Of all the wondrous world we see;
Its glow by day, its smile by night,
Are but reflections caught from thee;
Where’er we turn, thy glories shine,
And all things fair and bright are thine.

[VERSE 2]
When day, with farewell beam, delays
Among the opening clouds of even,
And we can almost think we gaze,
Through opening vistas, into heaven—
Those hues that mark the sun’s decline,
So soft, so radiant, Lord, are thine.

[VERSE 3]
When night, with wings of starry gloom,
O’ershadows all the earth and skies,
Like some dark, beauteous bird, whose plume
Is sparkling with unnumbered dyes—
That sacred gloom, those fires divine,
So grand, so countless, Lord, are thine.

[VERSE 4]
When youthful Spring around us breathes,
Thy Spirit warms her fragrant sigh;
And every flower that Summer wreathes
Is born beneath thy kindling eye;
Where’er we turn, thy glories shine,
And all things fair and bright are thine.

--- TITLE: There’s nothing bright, above, below [item: PG46041-63]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c63
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
There’s nothing bright, above, below,
From flowers that bloom to stars that glow,
But in its light my soul can see
Some features of the Deity.

[VERSE 2]
There’s nothing dark below, above,
But in its gloom I trace thy love,
And meekly wait the moment when
Thy touch shall make all bright again.

[VERSE 3]
The light, the dark, where’er I look,
Shall be one pure and shining book,
Where I may read, in words of flame,
The glories of thy wondrous name.

--- TITLE: Awaked from sin’s delusive sleep [item: PG46041-342]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c342
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
Awaked from sin’s delusive sleep,
My heavy guilt I feel, and weep;
Beneath a weight of woes oppressed,
I come to thee, my Lord, for rest.

[VERSE 2]
Now, from thy throne of grace above,
Look down upon my soul in love;
That smile shall sweeten all my pain,
And make my soul rejoice again.

[VERSE 3]
By thy divine, transforming power,
My ruined nature now restore;
And let my life and temper shine,
In blest resemblance, Lord! to thine.

--- TITLE: Since first thy word awaked my heart [item: PG46041-407]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c407
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
Since first thy word awaked my heart,
Like light new dawning o’er me,
Where’er I turn my eyes, thou art
All light and love before me.

[VERSE 2]
Naught else I feel, or hear, or see,
All bonds of earth I sever;
Thee, O my Lord, and only thee,
I live for, now, and ever.

[VERSE 3]
Like him whose fetters dropped away
When light shone o’er his prison,
My soul now touched by mercy’s ray,
Hath from its chains arisen.

[VERSE 4]
And shall the soul thou bidd’st be free,
Return to bondage? Never;
Thee, O my God, and only thee,
I live for, now, and ever.

--- TITLE: Like morning—when her early breeze [item: PG46041-409]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c409
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
Like morning—when her early breeze
Breaks up the surface of the seas,
That, in their furrows, dark with night,
Her hand may sow the seeds of light—

[VERSE 2]
Thy grace can send its breathings o’er
The spirit dark and lost before;
And, freshening all its depths, prepare
For truth divine to enter there.

[VERSE 3]
Till David touched his sacred lyre,
In silence lay the unbreathing wire;
But when he swept its chords along,
Then angels stooped to hear the song.

[VERSE 4]
So sleeps the soul, till thou, O Lord,
Shall deign to touch its lifeless chord;
Till, waked by thee, its breath shall rise,
In music worthy of the skies.

--- TITLE: The dove, let loose in eastern skies [item: PG46041-818]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c818
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
The dove, let loose in eastern skies,
Returning fondly home,
Ne’er stoops to earth her wing, nor flies
Where idle warblers roam;—

[VERSE 2]
But high she shoots through air and light
Above all low delay,
Where nothing earthly bounds her flight,
Nor shadow dims her way.

[VERSE 3]
So grant me, Lord, from every snare
And stain of passion free,
Aloft, through faith’s serener air,
To urge my course to thee;—

[VERSE 4]
No sin to cloud, no lure to stay,
My soul as home she springs;
Thy sunshine on her joyful way
Thy freedom on her wings.

--- TITLE: O thou who driest the mourner’s tear [item: PG46041-1005]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1005
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
O thou who driest the mourner’s tear,
How dark this world would be,
If, when deceived and wounded here,
We could not fly to thee.

[VERSE 2]
But thou wilt heal the broken heart,
Which, like the plants that throw
Their fragrance from the wounded part,
Breathes sweetness out of woe.

[VERSE 3]
When joy no longer soothes or cheers,
And e’en the hope that threw
A moment’s sparkle o’er our tears
Is dimmed and vanished too;

[VERSE 4]
O, who would bear life’s stormy doom,
Did not thy wing of love
Come brightly wafting through the gloom,
Our peace-branch from above?

[VERSE 5]
Then sorrow, touched by thee, grows bright
With more than rapture’s ray;
The darkness shows us worlds of light
We never saw by day.

--- TITLE: As down in the sunless retreats of the ocean [item: PG46041-1032]
--- SOURCE: PG46041 https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46041/pg46041-images.html#c1032
--- AUTHOR: Moore.

[VERSE 1]
As down in the sunless retreats of the ocean,
Sweet flowers are springing no mortal can see,
So deep in my heart, the still prayer of devotion
Unheard by the world, rises silent to thee—
My God! silent to thee—
Pure, warm, silent to thee.

[VERSE 2]
As still to the star of its worship, though clouded,
The needle points faithfully o’er the dim sea,
So, dark as I roam thro’ this wintery world shrouded,
The hope of my spirit turns trembling to thee—
My God! trembling to thee—
True, fond, trembling to thee.
