--- TITLE: O Thou who wouldst not have [item: PG76498-338]
--- SOURCE: PG76498 https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/76498.txt.utf-8 (numbered entry 338; browser source line 15676)
--- AUTHOR: Charles Wesley, 1749, a.

[VERSE 1]
O Thou who wouldst not have
One wretched sinner die,
Who diedst Thyself my soul to save
From endless misery,
Teach me my course to run,
While yet I sojourn here,
That when Thou comest on Thy throne
I may with joy appear.

[VERSE 2]
Thou art Thyself the Way,
Thyself in me reveal;
So shall I pass my life’s short day
Obedient to Thy will;
So shall I love my God
Because He first loved me,
And praise Thee in Thy bright abode
Through all eternity.

--- TITLE: In weariness and pain [item: PG76498-504]
--- SOURCE: PG76498 https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/76498.txt.utf-8 (numbered entry 504; browser source line 22250)
--- AUTHOR: Charles Wesley, 1749, a.

[VERSE 1]
In weariness and pain,
By sins and fears opprest,
I turn me to my Rest again,
My soul’s eternal Rest.

[VERSE 2]
The Lamb that died for me
And still my load doth bear;
To Jesus’ streaming wounds I flee
And find my quiet there.

[VERSE 3]
Jesus, was ever grief,
Was ever love, like Thine?
Thy sorrow, Lord, is my relief,
Thy life hath ransomed mine.

[VERSE 4]
O may I rise with Thee
And soar to things above
And spend a blest eternity
In praise of dying love!
