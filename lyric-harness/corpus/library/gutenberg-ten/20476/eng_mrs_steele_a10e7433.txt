--- TITLE: Great God, this sacred day of thine [item: PG20476-10]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0010
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Steele

[VERSE 1]
Great God, this sacred day of thine
Demands our souls' collected powers.
May we employ in work divine
These solemn, these devoted hours;
O may our souls adoring own
The grace which calls us to thy throne.

[VERSE 2]
Hence, ye vain cares and trifles, fly;
Where God resides appear no more;
Omniscient God, thy piercing eye
Can every secret thought explore:
O may thy grace our hearts refine,
And fix our thoughts on things divine.

[VERSE 3]
The word of life dispensed to-day
Invites us to a heavenly feast.
May every ear the call obey;
Be every heart a humble guest;
O bid the wretched sons of need
On soul-reviving dainties feed.

[VERSE 4]
Thy spirit's powerful aid impart;
O may thy word, with life divine,
Engage the ear, and warm the heart;
Then shall the day indeed be thine;
Then shall our souls adoring own
The grace which calls us to thy throne.

--- TITLE: My Father! cheering name [item: PG20476-102]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0102
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Steele

[VERSE 1]
My Father! cheering name!
O, may I call thee mine?
Give me the humble hope to claim
A portion so divine.

[VERSE 2]
Whate'er thy will denies,
I calmly would resign;
For thou art just, and good, and wise:
O, bend my will to thine!

[VERSE 3]
Whate'er thy will ordains,
O give me strength to bear
Still let me know a father reigns,
And trust a father's care.

[VERSE 4]
Thy ways are little known
To my weak, erring sight;
Yet shall my soul, believing, own
That all thy ways are right.

[VERSE 5]
My Father!--blissful name!
Above expression dear!
If thou accept my humble claim,
I bid adieu to fear.

--- TITLE: My Maker and my King [item: PG20476-124]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0124
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Steele

[VERSE 1]
My Maker and my King!
To thee my all I owe:
Thy sovereign bounty is the spring,
From whence my blessings flow.

[VERSE 2]
Thou ever good and kind!
A thousand reasons move,
A thousand obligations bind
My heart to grateful love.

[VERSE 3]
The creature of thy hand,
On thee alone I live:
My God! thy benefits demand
More praise than tongue can give.

[VERSE 4]
O let thy grace inspire
My soul with strength divine;
Let all my powers to thee aspire,
And all my days be thine.

--- TITLE: Ah wretched souls, who strive in vain [item: PG20476-443]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0443
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Steele

[VERSE 1]
Ah wretched souls, who strive in vain,
Slaves to the world, and slaves to sin!
A nobler toil may I sustain,
A nobler satisfaction win.

[VERSE 2]
May I resolve, with all my heart,
With all my powers, to serve the Lord;
Nor from his precepts e'er depart,
Whose service is a rich reward.

[VERSE 3]
Be this the purpose of my soul,
My solemn, my determined choice,
To yield to his supreme control,
And in his kind commands rejoice.

[VERSE 4]
O may I never faint nor tire,
Nor, wandering, leave his sacred ways;
Great God! accept my soul's desire,
And give me strength to live thy praise.

--- TITLE: Far from these scenes of night [item: PG20476-559]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0559
--- AUTHOR: Mrs. Steele

[VERSE 1]
Far from these scenes of night
Unbounded glories rise,
And realms of infinite delight,
Unknown to mortal eyes.

[VERSE 2]
No cloud those regions know,
Forever bright and fair;
For sin, the source of mortal woe,
Can never enter there.

[VERSE 3]
There night is never known,
Nor sun's faint, sickly ray;
But glory from th' eternal throne
Spreads everlasting day.

[VERSE 4]
O may this prospect fire
Our hearts with ardent love!
And lively faith and strong desire
Bear every thought above.
