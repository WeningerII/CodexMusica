--- TITLE: Stretched on the cross, the Saviour dies [item: PG20476-232]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0232
--- AUTHOR: Steele

[VERSE 1]
Stretched on the cross, the Saviour dies,
Hark! his expiring groans arise;
See, from his hands, his feet, his side,
Descends the sacred, crimson tide.

[VERSE 2]
And didst thou bleed?--for sinners bleed?
And could the sun behold the deed?
No; he withdrew his cheering ray,
And darkness veiled the mourning day.

[VERSE 3]
Can I survey this scene of woe,
Where mingling grief and mercy flow,
And yet my heart so hard remain,--
Unmoved by either love or pain!

[VERSE 4]
Come, dearest Lord, thy grace impart,
To warm this cold, this stupid heart,
Till all its powers and passions move,
In melting grief and ardent love.

--- TITLE: Jesus demands this heart of mine [item: PG20476-329]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0329
--- AUTHOR: Steele

[VERSE 1]
Jesus demands this heart of mine,
Demands my love, my joy, my care,
But ah, how dead to things divine,
How cold my best affections are!

[VERSE 2]
'Tis sin, alas! with dreadful power,
Divides my Saviour from my sight;
O, for one happy, shining hour
Of sacred freedom, sweet delight!

[VERSE 3]
Come, gracious Lord; thy love can raise
My captive powers from sin and death,
And fill my heart and life with praise,
And tune my last, expiring breath.

--- TITLE: Religion can assuage [item: PG20476-408]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0408
--- AUTHOR: Steele

[VERSE 1]
Religion can assuage
The tempest of the soul;
And every fear shall lose its rage
At her divine control.

[VERSE 2]
Through life's bewildered way,
Her hand unerring leads;
And o'er the path her heavenly ray
A cheering lustre sheds.

[VERSE 3]
When reason, tired and blind,
Sinks helpless and afraid,
Thou blest supporter of the mind,
How powerful is thine aid!

[VERSE 4]
O, let us feel thy power,
And find thy sweet relief,
To brighten every gloomy hour
And soften every grief.

--- TITLE: When fainting in the sultry waste [item: PG20476-453]
--- SOURCE: PG20476 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20476/20476-h/20476-h.htm#h0453
--- AUTHOR: Steele

[VERSE 1]
When fainting in the sultry waste,
And parched with thirst extreme,
The weary pilgrim longs to taste
The cool, refreshing stream.

[VERSE 2]
So longs the weary, fainting mind,
Oppressed with sins and woes,
Some soul-reviving spring to find,
Whence heavenly comfort flows.

[VERSE 3]
O, may I thirst for thee, my God,
With ardent, strong desire;
And still, through all this desert road,
To taste thy grace aspire.

[VERSE 4]
Then shall my prayer to thee ascend,
A grateful sacrifice;
My mourning voice thou wilt attend,
And grant me full supplies.
