# Where the time goes in one song

## 1. The bill, in three units that must not be added together

**One whole song attempt (round 10, run 33266613606, job 99137676797) — the only end-to-end wall clock anyone measured.** Step wall 17:49:14.358Z → 18:11:29.508Z = **1335.15s**. Server-side turn `ms` sums to **1033.738s (77.4%)**: t0 502.401, t1 301.918, t2 133.055, t3 80.780, t4 15.195, t5 0.389. The remaining **301.41s is client-side**, dominated by ≥240s of retry backoff (4 × the 60s floor at `scripts/flash_battery.mjs:260`). Eleven `lyric_revise` calls total. The three revise-only turns give a live mean of **103.2s per fold** (515.753s / 5 calls) — but that figure includes Gemini hop latency, so it is an upper bound on harness cost, not a harness measurement. The run produced no finished song.

**One harness call, measured two ways, on two shapes.** Both lanes ran `revise … --propose=defer:STATE`:

- Cold fold 1, 22 lines / 24 groups / 60 mandated pairs, no blueprint: **59.197 / 59.407 / 60.233s** bare, rc=4, one question emitted. `state.json` shows `answered=0` — nothing was replayed.
- Warm-process resume, 14-line couplet shape: **20.31–23.21s** with the M-167 replay memo on, **21.27–36.74s** with it off.

**Inside one call — the itemized bill.** Fold lane, profiled shares over a 158.09s profile at 2.65× overhead (call counts identical across two profile runs; cumtimes agree within 1.1%, so the run is deterministic and the spread is the box):

| component | profiled share | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `modal_field`→`joint_field`→`_field_one`→`candidates`→`score` (revise.py:2789-2790, 116 calls → 37 distinct field builds) | **69.4%** | see §2 |
| slop floor (`floor.py:1299`→`features.py:433`→`_predictability`→`features.field`) | 13.7% | necessary — produced 2 of the 4 emitted findings |
| 77-schema relation layer (`grade`×4, `whole_vocabulary_pairs`×4, `line_pairs_for`×77, `evaluate`×207,159) | 12.0% | necessary, and **already deduplicated** — 3 of the 4 calls hit `relations._WVP_MEMO` and did no work |
| phonetic data loading — **five** separate `Lexicon.__init__` per process | 3.6% (~1.7s bare) | 4 of 5 are recomputation |
| meter / shape / function / readability / sentencehood | <1% combined | necessary and essentially free |
| process startup + import (measured outside the profiler) | 0.485–0.499s = **0.8% of the bare fold** | not the problem, by two orders of magnitude |

Cross-cutting context row, already inside rows 1 and 3 and **not to be added to them**: `lyric_harness.py:3209 score()`, 101.0s cumulative over **2,360,149 calls**. Two point three six million pairwise rhyme scorings to ask the writer to shorten one 13-syllable line.

The one flag the entire 59.5s fold produced — `DENSITY_OUT_OF_BAND`, "L3: 13 syllable(s) — outside the calibrated [5, 12] band" — came from the near-free tier. That is the audit in one line: the answer cost microseconds, the run cost a minute.

**The same seconds through the cache lens** (ladder lane, 14-line shape, `scratchpad/floor.py`, 3 reps): `Reviser()` construction 2.90/2.92/3.12s; **first `brief()` on empty instance caches 14.46/14.47/16.57s**; the *same* brief once those caches are warm **0.38/0.38/0.40s** — 38× less; `inspect()` warm 0.20–0.22s; `verify()` of the one folded answer 2.04/2.05/3.85s. Sum 21.43s against measured memo-on resumes of 20.31–23.21s: the reconstruction lands within 3%.

**The lanes do not disagree — they are looking at the same money.** The ~14.1s of "cache filling" the ladder lane charges to the first `brief()` *is* the `joint_field`/`_field_one`/`candidates` work the fold lane charges to row 1. Two lenses, two different remedies: don't compute it (fold lane) versus compute it once and keep it (ladder lane). Fixing either shrinks the other's prize. Do not sum them.

Where absolute seconds matter, the fold lane is better supported: it used the shape round 10 actually drew (seed 10, 22 lines, 24 groups, 60 pairs), three cold repeats, and a bare wall clock alongside the profile. The ladder lane shrank to 14 lines to fit its budget and says so; its value is the resume slope and the warm/cold cache split, not the absolute size of a fold.

## 2. The thing that undercuts the headline, stated before the ranking

The 69.4% row is measured on a draft where **nearly every mandated pair PASSES**. `revise.py:2736` `continue`s past every non-passing verdict before reaching the two `modal_field` calls at :2789-2790; 116 calls over 60 pairs means ~58 pairs passed. Round 10's drafts were the opposite regime — 20 of 23 lines unresolved, so most verdicts short-circuit and the doctrine-9 loop mostly does not fire. In that regime the field work goes instead into `brief()`'s per-flagged-line `joint_field` calls at `revise.py:3871`, which are **necessary**: they build the candidate menu the writer reads.

So the honest statement of the largest measured item is: *the candidate-field machinery is 65–70% of a cold harness call on both measured shapes; on a near-clean draft that spend goes to a note-severity check that cannot reject anything and whose expensive half is discarded, and on a stuck draft it goes to the menus the writer actually needs.* **Nobody measured a fold on a heavily-flagged draft.** Both lanes measured the clean regime. That is the single largest gap in this audit, and it means the biggest measured saving is worth the most exactly when a run is nearly done and least when it is stuck — which is when the seconds are actually being burned.

## 3. Confirmed losses, ranked — measured above unmeasured

**1. The MODAL_RHYME pair scan builds two complete lexicon-wide candidate fields per mandated pair and throws the expensive half away.** `revise.py:2789-2790` is `_, forbidden_i = self.modal_field(wi, …)` — the `offered` list, which is what `field_depth=None` makes a full-pool build, is bound to `_`; the next two lines ask only `wj in forbidden_i` / `wi in forbidden_j`, two membership tests on words already in hand.
*Size:* **MEASURED** at 69.4% of a 158.09s profile on a 22-line clean-regime fold — ~41s of the 59.5s bare, subject to the profiler's per-call bias (this row is 2.36M tiny calls, so its true bare share is somewhat below 69.4% by an amount not measured) and subject to §2's regime caveat. Three independent facts make it waste on that draft: it emitted zero `MODAL_RHYME` and zero `HOMEOTELEUTON`; its own severity is `note` and the prompt states "New NOTES do not reject", so it cannot move a verdict; the costly half is discarded at its own call site.
*Answer-preserving?* **Conditionally, and unproven.** `forbidden = homeo + rest_ranked[:k]` and every member came through `_field_one`, whose predicate is per-pair, so `wj in forbidden_i` implies that pair passes `admits()` — one scalar test, ~40µs, in place of two full-pool builds. This is answer-preserving **only while `rd.field_depth is None`**; at a finite depth the field is a truncation of a score-ordered pool, membership stops being a per-pair property, and the shortcut would silently stop emitting notes the full build would have emitted. That is a changed verdict. The fix must be gated on `field_depth is None` and refuse otherwise. Nobody implemented or tested it.

**2. Every CLI call rebuilds the Reviser and refills its instance caches, in the same worker process, for the same conversation.** `lyric_harness.py:9847` hands `RM.wrap` a Reviser built fresh for this call; three successive `Reviser()` calls in one process cost 3.15/2.46/2.09s, so nothing is shared across instances.
*Size:* **MEASURED on the 14-line shape** at ~17.5s of a 21.4s reconstruction (14.5s cold-cache brief where the warm one is 0.38s, plus 2.9s construction) — ~80% of a resume. This is roughly 6× what M-167 recovered. It is largely the same seconds as loss 1 seen from the other side.
*Answer-preserving?* **Not established, and I will not call it a speedup.** Two things must be proved first: that every cache key is complete over declared coordinates (`_anchor_cache` is `(word, promote)`; `_field_cache`/`_matrix_cache` keys were read but not audited against `decl`/`rdecl`/`profile`), and that the hard clear-at-N eviction (`_field_cache` `clear()` past 64, `_matrix_cache` past 8) cannot make a result depend on what a *previous request* put in the cache. If any cache can return a different answer to an identical call because of cross-request history, that is worth more than the 17.5s. There is also a memory gate: `check_deploy_memory.js` measured 882MB peak on a 2048MB instance with M-157's OOM history reaching the transcript as bare 502s.

**3. The deferred replay re-grades every intermediate draft on every resume.**
*Size:* **MEASURED, both arms, two replicates.** Memo off: +2.991/+2.977s per resume (21.27→36.74). Memo on: +0.385/+0.383. 87.1% of the slope removed; whole 6-call conversation 166.9→132.7s (−20.5%); resume 5 alone 36.72→23.09s (−37.1%). The memo's disclosure at resume 5 reads "40 of 57 grading call(s) answered from the process memo."
*Answer-preserving?* **Yes, and verified empirically here:** all four replicates hash to the same digest `a523d5fa8736fad9` over every step's stdout, normalised only for the temp dir and the disclosure line. Not one byte of any verdict moved.
*Correction to the project's own framing, twice over:* (a) the memo is worth **~0.34s per replayed call, not ~15s** — memo-off replay was already warm-cheap, because the replay fills the Reviser's own caches as it goes; the memo replaces a warm recomputation with a lookup. (b) Fold 1 replays nothing and still costs 59.5s, so **the memo could not have saved one second of the floor under every deferred conversation.** It ranks third by size and first by readiness: it is the only item in this audit that is built, tested, and provably answer-preserving.

**4. `group_merges` re-grades the entire draft to read one field the caller is already holding.** `revise.py:1952` `rep = self.grade(lines, m, profile=profile)`; `:1954` reads `rep["collisions"]` and `rep` is never referenced again through :2017. `inspect()` already bound the same grade at `:2659`, 449 lines earlier. `group_merge` defaults to `"report"`, so this fires on every default run.
*Size:* **Call count confirmed and derivable without running** — exactly one extra `Reviser.grade` per `Reviser.inspect`. Seconds **UNMEASURED**, and this is where the two skeptics part company. On a no-blueprint run the two grades key identically and `_WVP_MEMO` absorbs the relation half entirely — which is exactly what the fold lane observed (3 of 4 `whole_vocabulary_pairs` calls did no work). On the blueprint-carrying `finish` path — the live path — `inspect()` passes `sections=_sections` and `group_merges` cannot, `_wvp_key` folds `sections` in, so the two calls **can never share the memo** and one `inspect()` pays two full 77-schema builds. The fold lane priced one build at ~17.6s profiled (~11% of the profile), which agrees with the repo's own independent figure of 19.9s/build at `relations.py:6480-6482`. So on the live path this is plausibly the **largest unmeasured item on the bill**, and it is invisible to the fold lane because that lane ran without a blueprint. The grading skeptic's deflation (the `_fan` is lazily gated at `revise.py:1601-1604`, so a draft with no violating verdict pays nothing) is correct — but an unconverged draft has violating verdicts by definition, so it does not deflate the runs that matter.
*Answer-preserving?* **Yes, provably, and it is the cleanest proof in the audit.** `collisions` (`:1796-1827`) reads only `matrix` (from the already-cached `_matrix`), `endwords`, `m.groups_of`, `m.requirement`, `m.in_scope`, `THETA_COLLISION` and `self._attribution`. It reads no `sections`, no `verdicts`, no `_stream`. Lift it into a helper; pass `collisions` in as an optional argument defaulting to computing its own (`group_merges` is public and called by `test_revise.py:1194` and `door_census`).

**5. `verify()` re-grades the unchanged `before` draft on every attempt, and briefs every flagged line to read two fields of the one line that changed.** `revise.py:3950` `brief(before)` (itself a whole-draft `inspect` plus a `joint_field` per flagged line), `:3952` `inspect(before)`, `:3955` `inspect(after)`. `changed` is computed at `:3941`, nine lines earlier; `grep` gives exactly two readers of `b_before`, both indexed by members of `changed`. M-167's memo is blind to it — `MemoReviser.verify` delegates to the real bound method, so the internal `self.brief`/`self.inspect` never touch the proxy, and the verify key is fresh on every attempt within a round.
*Size:* **UNMEASURED in seconds**, and bounded below what the claim implies: `_matrix(before)` is already cached, and the loop's own `brief()` at the round entry already warmed `_field_cache` for the same draft, so most of the repeated field work within one call is cache hits. The residual is the floor / readability / grade passes. The ladder lane's whole-`verify` measurement is 2.04–3.85s on 14 lines.
*Answer-preserving?* **Yes for narrowing `brief(before)` to `changed`** — each Brief is independent (the per-line body reads only whole-draft objects and the line index), and Briefs outside `changed` are provably never read. Not for hoisting `f_before` out of `verify` into the loop; that splits the rubric from the check.

**6. Fourteen of the schemas that actually execute run to completion and every instance they produce is discarded.** `whole_vocabulary_pairs` loops all 77; `line_pairs_for` drops every same-line instance at `relations.py:6903`; 20 schemas are confined to one line by `_frame_key`'s own rules, of which 14 pass the capability gate and run. Sharpened by the skeptic: the capability gate returns before `enumerate_spans`, so only 46 (prominence) to 53 (the `finish` path) schemas execute at all — the 14 are **26–30% of what runs**, not 18%. They carry 15 of the registry's 26 `any_token` rules and all 6 token-half rules, i.e. the branches whose cost is quadratic in tokens per line.
*Size:* **Structural share measured and reproduced** (`14` from a sub-100ms registry query). Wall-clock share **UNMEASURED**, bounded above by the relation layer's 12% of a fold.
*Answer-preserving?* Yes, with a predicate read off `schema.figure.frame` and `schema.placement` at call time — the same two coordinates `_frame_key` already reads, so it is mechanical, not a taxonomy. **One declared side effect:** `realise` raises `RuntimeError('candidate explosion')` past `max_pairs`, and pruning removes pairs that count toward it, so a draft that currently raises could stop raising. An error becoming a verdict is an observable change in the right direction — declare it, do not discover it.

**7. The declared-schema stream and its per-schema `realise()` are memoised by nothing.** Six rebuilds per `verify()` over at most 4 distinct `(lines, sections)` keys, × the distinct declared schemas (≤22 hard ceiling, ~16 expected at 28 draws). Seconds **UNMEASURED**; the ~0.8s/grade figure is refuted as a 77→22 extrapolation with no per-schema variance measured.
*Answer-preserving?* Only as a **result** memo keyed on lines + sections + bearing + canonical name. Memoising the **stream** is a defect: `revise.py:1279` calls `mark_refrain_tail(_stream, lines=_bearing)`, which mutates it, so a shared stream under a different bearing hands `epistrophe / radif` another mandate's refrain-tail frame.

**8. Five separate `Lexicon` constructions per process**, from module-level singletons that do not know about each other (`lyric_harness.py:6338`, `features.py:278`, `fit.py:566`, `morphology.py:265`, `phonology/eng.py:109`). **MEASURED:** 5.68s profiled / 3.6%, ~1.7s bare, four of five redundant. Trivially answer-preserving (identical immutable table); a real refactor for ~1.4s.

**9. Freebies, provably inert.** `joint_field` walks `ranked` twice evaluating the same pure `_spelled_rime` on every candidate, the second pass computing the exact complement of the first. **MEASURED** by the skeptic at 2.04µs/call over a 4,000-call loop → ~8–16ms per `joint_field` call at the repo's own field sizes. Not a bill item; a free cleanup. Same for a `_spelled_rime` memo keyed on the word alone (it reads no declaration coordinate, unlike `_word_anchors` which correctly carries `promote`).

**10. `worker.py` is not in the deployed image.** `mcp/Dockerfile:20` is `COPY mcp/*.js ./mcp/`; `git ls-files mcp/ | grep -v '\.js$'` shows `worker.py` is the one runtime file that glob misses; `render.yaml` declares no `LYRIC_WORKER`, so the warm path is attempted and fails on every call (spawn exits 2 in ~20ms, `_killWorker()` rejects, `runVerb` falls back cold). Dockerfile last touched 2026-08-18; `worker.py` landed 2026-08-28; no CI check reads the Dockerfile's file list. **Real, unrecorded deployment defect.** Its size, however, is refuted — see §4.

## 4. What was killed

The audit had teeth. Roughly half the readers' headline claims did not survive.

**"Turn 0's 502s of work was thrown away at turn 1."** Refuted by measurement. The transcript's `sizes.lyric` drops 17,858 → 756 bytes, which reads like a wiped record. It is not: modelling the exact quantity the battery logs gives 143 bytes for a zero-record state, 773 for five records. **756 is five retained answers** — exactly the five that turn 0's six exit-4 folds produce. The shrink is `pending` being emptied at a stop condition, which the harness comment the claim itself quotes says outright. **0s lost.** This is the cleanest "a reader's story was wrong" in the set, and it is also the argument for the one free fix in §6: `answers_on_record` is already computed at `lyric_tools.js:1085/1109` and dropped by `chat.js:402-407`. Had it been in the transcript, this claim would never have been written.

**"The missing worker.py costs ~700s a round."** Mechanism confirmed, size collapsed to single-digit-to-low-tens of seconds. M-155 shipped *two* builds and only one is missing: `relations._WVP_MEMO` lives in `quality/relations.py`, which `Dockerfile:31` copies wholesale, so the intra-process dedupe (the larger win, 59.9s→43.9s on a grade) **is deployed**. The 92s→32s bench was a *shallow* fold; M-167 exists precisely because the warm worker did **not** flatten the replay slope. `_WVP_MEMO_CAP=32` with FIFO eviction decays the cross-call carry exactly as folds deepen. And the ladder lane corroborates independently: a warm process per se buys **0.03s** (the import), because the Reviser is rebuilt anyway.

**"The retry budget cost 255s (19.1% of the round)."** Refuted, and the sign is inverted. With `retries` reset per turn, turn 4 still spends the same ≥240s of backoff — it had the full budget under both policies. Turn 4's own 15.195s was wasted because the model abandoned the song, a writer cause. The scoping's only effect was turn 5's 389ms 429. **~0.4s.** Fixing it *adds* up to 240s to a round like this one. It is a reliability fix that buys turns by spending seconds, not a wall-clock saving.

**"The first-draft brief tells the writer 'lines 1.T7 & 2.T4 rhyme'."** Refuted. `plan.py:3046-3055` is a branch, and since M-117 the planner draws a relation per group, so the majority branch prints "stand in \<schema\> — a NAMED relation, judged as itself, **NOT AS PLAIN RHYME**". Counted over the two post-M-117 banked songs: 26 of 35 rows (74%) take the relation branch; the claimed sentence covers ~26%. The 391/489 statistic is not reproducible (337/406 = 83.0% over the mandates that exist in that file). A legend *does* sit in the same model's context at `lyric_tools.js:791`. And M-91 already fixed the *revise* prompt, so "stays violated through every subsequent round" cannot follow. What survives is small and unregistered: `slots.word_phrase` is never called from `plan.py`.

**"The candidate field materialises the whole pool when consumers read at most 30."** Refuted on its specifics — `homeo` is unbounded and read entire; `_field_cache` already collapses the doctrine-9 loop to one build per *distinct* word (the fold lane measured exactly this working: **116 calls → 37 distinct builds**); the index is 18,010 entries, not 40k. But note what happened: the reader was refuted for the right reasons **and was pointing at the right place**. `loop.py:957`'s own comment — "a rebuild of the candidate field, which is where a run's time actually goes" — turns out to be correct, and the fold lane is the first measurement in this repo to confirm it. The cache the skeptic cited as the refutation is measured working, and the cost survives it.

**"65 of 94 `enumerate_spans` invocations are duplicated."** Refuted as stated: that is a *registry census* presented as a per-call measurement — the tell is that the claim's "per-call query" returns the identical pair. Schemas whose capabilities the stream does not supply refuse before enumerating. The true per-call figure on the `finish` path is **42 of 66 (64%) over 24 distinct rules**. The ratio survives; every absolute the claim derived is computed off the wrong base.

**"Tier 2's silent zero-proposal exits burn rounds re-deriving dead ends."** Refuted: `MemoReviser.joint_field` keys on `("joint", calls, exclude, profile)` with no draft and no round, so an unchanged dead end is answered from the memo on rounds 2–4; and five of the seven named exits `continue` *before* the first `joint_field`. The proposed fix moves `stop_reason` from ROUND_LIMIT to NO_PROGRESS — a reporting change, which by rule 2 is a defect and not a speedup.

**"The `(line_no, attempt)` replay key means rounds 2+ ask nothing new."** Refuted. A line accepted at attempt k and re-flagged later replays text now *in* the draft, `changed` is empty, verify returns "nothing was fixed", attempt 1 misses and **suspends** — a new question. And round 10 completed four rounds without suspending, which the claimed mechanism cannot produce. Narrow survivor: a line that exhausts its attempts in round 1 *without* acceptance is asked nothing new in any later round.

**"The 43.6s vs 0.1s inspect."** Three readers leaned on this. It was measured on a **four-line fixture**, in commit 58c8b51 whose own subject is "WIP CHECKPOINT — work NOT verified", **twelve days before** the 77-schema layer entered `grade()`. Both timing lanes now supersede it: a cold-Reviser brief is 14.5s and a warm re-brief 0.38s on 14 lines. Retire the anchor.

**The audit's own refuted hypothesis:** the transport reader expected the driver's 130s `--pace` to dominate the 22.5 minutes (5 inter-turn sleeps + 4 backoffs ≈ 1170s of 1335s). The arithmetic killed it — only 301.41s is client-side at all, forcing this dispatch's pace to roughly 10s. The pacing was not the story; the harness compute was.

## 5. Still UNMEASURED, and the one command that settles each

1. **Per-component *bare* seconds** absent profiler distortion. There are profiled shares and one bare total, not per-component bare timings. → On a scratch copy of the tree, stub `revise.py:2789-2790` and `time` the same fold three times, then `cmp` full stdout against baseline. One experiment gives both the size and the answer-preservation check.
2. **A blueprint fold.** Every fold-lane number is a no-blueprint run; the live `finish` path always declares one, which turns on the meter/function layers *and* splits the relation memo key (loss 4). → Re-run the fold recipe with `--blueprint=` from a drawn plan; diff the profile.
3. **A fold on a heavily-flagged draft** — the regime round 10 was actually in (§2). → Same recipe on a draft with several RHYME-flagged lines; read `modal_field` ncalls against `_field_one` ncalls. The ratio is the share of note-scan builds `brief()` would have needed anyway.
4. **Whether `features.field` and `Reviser._field_one` compute the same admission predicate.** The floor makes 514,298 of the run's 2.36M `score()` calls through its *own* field. → Read `features.field`'s rule. If identical, a shared cache is worth ~13.7%; if not, sharing it is exactly the silent-comparator substitution doctrine 1 forbids.
5. **Cross-request Reviser safety** (the ~17.5s prize, loss 2). → Not a timing question first. Audit the four cache keys for completeness against `decl`/`rdecl`/`profile`, then run the full ladder twice — fresh Reviser per request vs held — and `cmp` full stdout at every step.
6. **The resume slope at 22–28 lines and out to resume 10+** (the reported 340–515s single resumes). → `arm.py 22 10` in both arms. The +2.98s/resume slope is shape-dependent; do not carry it.
7. **How much of round 10's 1033.7s is harness vs Gemini hops.** → Instrument `runVerb` to log per-call ms, or project the per-hop timing `chat.js` already holds, and re-dispatch.
8. **`group_merges`' extra relation build on a blueprint run** (loss 4's live-path size). → `python3 -m cProfile -s cumtime lyric_harness.py song BP.json DRAFT --groups=… --relations=…` and read **`build_stream` ncalls** — that counts real builds and excludes memo hits, which is the number that matters. `whole_vocabulary_pairs` ncalls will read 2 whether or not the second call costs anything.
9. **Worker RSS across a whole song**, before the Dockerfile fix ships. 181MB after one light call is not the steady state of a worker holding the candidate machinery plus 32 memo entries on a 2048MB instance, and M-157's OOMs reach the transcript as bare 502s.
10. **Whether those rounds could converge.** → No new run needed: read run 33266613606's job log for the per-round `LoopResult` block, count `tier2 (0 tried)` rows per round, and count accepted attempts whose reason contains "disclosing" and names a pursued note against the lines re-opened in the next round.

## 6. Recommended order of work

**First, deploy M-167's replay memo.** It is third by size and first by readiness: built, tested, −20.5% on a six-call conversation and −37% at resume 5, with four replicates hashing to one digest. It is the only item here that is finished and provably answer-preserving. Ship it, and correct the story that goes with it — it is worth ~0.34s per replayed call, and it does not touch the ~18s constant floor under every resume.

**Second, measure before cutting the largest item.** Two runs (§5 items 1 and 3) settle whether the 69.4% note scan is recoverable in the regime that actually consumes the time. If the flagged-draft fold shows `modal_field` firing rarely, the headline shrinks and the effort belongs elsewhere. Do this before writing the gate, not after.

**Third, `group_merges`' collisions helper.** One extra whole-draft `grade` per `inspect`, unconditionally at the default, plus a second full relation build per distinct draft on the blueprint path the connector always takes. The proof is the tightest in the audit: `collisions` reads only the already-cached matrix, the endwords and the mandate. Confirm the size with `build_stream` ncalls first (§5 item 8) — this is the best cost-per-unit-of-effort candidate remaining.

**Fourth, narrow `verify()`'s `brief(before)` to `changed`.** Provable by grep — two readers, both indexed by members of `changed`. Small warm, but it also stops minting field builds on a cold Reviser.

**Fifth, prune the intra-line-confined schemas** with a predicate read off `figure.frame`/`placement` at call time, declaring the candidate-explosion side effect.

**Sixth, the freebies:** single-pass `_spelled_rime` partition, a `_spelled_rime` memo keyed on the word, and unifying the five `Lexicon` singletons (~1.4s, plus ~10ms per `joint_field` call). Provably inert, cheap.

**Seventh, the Dockerfile — after the RSS measurement, not before.** And `chat.js`'s trace projection: add the already-computed `answers_on_record` and `args.seed`. Zero seconds; it is the change that would have prevented one of this audit's refuted claims from being written.

**Not recommended, and why — the biggest win is inadmissible.** Holding the Reviser and its caches across requests attacks ~17.5s of a ~21.4s resume, roughly 6× what M-167 recovered. It is the largest lever in this audit and I am not recommending it, because nobody proved that those cache keys are complete over the declaration or that the wholesale clear-at-N eviction cannot make a result depend on what a previous request left behind. A field cache that can return a different answer to an identical call is worth less than the seconds it saves; this repo grades songs. Prove it first (§5 item 5), then it becomes the top item.

Also inadmissible as speedups, for the record: any `joint_field` memo above `_field_cache` with a lazily-spelled key (that is the M-139 defect this repo already paid for); memoising the relation *stream* rather than its result (`mark_refrain_tail` mutates it); threading `sections` into `group_merges` to force a memo hit (that changes which pairs get rescued under the stanza frame — a semantic decision); promoting `HOMEOTELEUTON` to a flag or counting pursued notes against `allow_net_new` (locked by mutation QR1; it changes `verify()`'s acceptance set); the per-turn retry reset (worth doing, but it *adds* wall clock); disclosing tier 2's silent exits (worth doing, but it changes `stop_reason`); and the prompt-rendering repairs (`word_phrase` in `plan.py`, the inverted `slot_conflict` sentence at `propose.py:552`, which tells a writer with a first-word and an end-word obligation that its line is unfixable and the fault lies with the mandate). Those last are real defects worth fixing on their merits, they cost zero seconds, and because they change what the writer is *asked*, the honest test is a preregistered same-seed A/B on `unresolved` at stop — not a speedup claim.
