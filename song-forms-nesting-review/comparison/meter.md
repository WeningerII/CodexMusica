# Song Forms and poetic meter

**The explorer can show a documented metrical expectation for a form. Deciding how a particular line scans is a separate operation.** All 364 catalog forms describe types. A performance, an edition of a poem, a pronunciation, and an analyst's reading are occurrences with their own evidence. A meter reference attached to a diagram should say which part it concerns, what unit it measures, which reading it assumes and whether the association is required, typical, optional or disputed.

## The 203-entry crosswalk

[meter-crosswalk.tsv](meter-crosswalk.tsv) covers every original ID from M001 through M203 in order. The names and IDs come from the team's catalog. Its three positionally joined matrices distinguish attestation, proposed representation and operational evidence. The TSV applies the six-row v2 corrections and then v3, including the 32 representability and 13 operations updates. The eight compact evidence fields use the later fixed research snapshot's legend. Rows also link subsequent source discussions where the IDs are mentioned. Those links are provenance. They do not silently alter the snapshot or confer fixture approval. All three matrices and all 203 rows were checked for exact ID alignment. The crosswalk contains **203 research IDs, 87 with a selected catalog comparison and 116 without one**. [meter-links.tsv](meter-links.tsv) expands the 87 into **105 distinct ID/form pairs**. Each pairing records its kind and strength, its measured unit (for example feet per line), its position (for example line 9), an explanation of its scope, catalog and research locators, and the evidence for that particular link. All 105 have a catalog locator. For 52 pairs the comparison rests on the catalog alone. The other 53 cite supplemental scope evidence, which can be a bounded research packet, a publication or a specific scanned occurrence. Each source supports only the scope described. The 63 linked IDs whose frozen v3 attestation is “unverified” retain that status. A link, even one added using later evidence, cannot upgrade it.

This is an illustrative meter-first index. It cannot determine every meter that governs a catalog form. M033, for instance, illustrates pentameter with Heroic couplet. Sicilian tercet, Heroic quatrain, Venus and Adonis stanza, Rhyme royal, Ottava rima and the opening lines of a Spenserian stanza would need distinct associations for an exhaustive reverse lookup. Every ID is preserved, including those without an example in the displayed models. A missing link says nothing about what has occurred in English verse. The package validator reads the ID table to check title references and count IDs. The interactive explorer consumes neither TSV, and diagram nodes have labels and children without stable node IDs. The position column locates the association within the form. The governed_unit column states the measured dimension or, for a comparison with no fixed meter, what the link can describe. A future overlay would need a complete, verified inventory for its advertised coverage and a stable way to address model nodes.

The frozen v3 attestation statuses include 60 verified for the stated claim, 137 unverified and six with individually qualified statuses across the full inventory. The later fixed snapshot flagged 94 IDs without a mapped scan or analysis. Neither count measures functioning recognition. **Reading the tables.** Each pair is a counterpart, component, example or comparison. The ID summary marks heterogeneous groups as mixed and empty groups as none. Strength describes that specific relation. “Defining” concerns the catalog specification, “typical” permits variants, “variant-specific” applies only to the named branch, “optional” covers licensed variation, and “illustrative” and “occurrence-specific” distinguish a comparison from a located instance. Evidence basis is a separate column. Frozen research status always belongs to the ID.

## What the two repository snapshots actually accept

These comparisons use the historical main snapshot and the separate Stage 4 review head. Both contain byte-identical rhyme-scheme and writing-workflow modules. The [paired checks and replay instructions](../Andrej-meter.md) identify which assertions were executed and which paths were read.

**Onegin stanza.** The reviewed diagram’s local letters encode both rhyme and the stress at the line ending. In `aBaB ccDD eFFe GG`, lowercase marks a trailing unstressed syllable and uppercase marks a stressed ending. The inventory’s original legend reverses that mapping. In both engine revisions mixed case activates *refrain* notation: the parser instead requires lines 2/4, 7/8, 10/11 and 13/14 to repeat verbatim. Uppercasing the whole string gives the intended rhyme-only partition, `ABABCCDDEFFEGG`, which the engine does not name. It names the different partition `ABABCBCDCDEDEE` “Onegin stanza”. A caller must keep the end-stress vector separate from the rhyme specification. **Generated rhyme.** A one-line section gets a singleton. Every generated scheme for a sung section of two or more lines contains a rhyme group with at least two members. The planner requires at least one such section per song and reuses one scheme shape per function. Groups are instantiated within the section, with separate identity returns for repeated material. **Burden-first carol and Extended AABA.** The 22-function vocabulary includes the relevant names. That does not make every catalog arrangement generatable or verified. In both revisions, a section explicitly typed `burden` must follow a verse immediately. The opening burden in the catalog carol triggers `SECTION_NOT_ADJACENT` at position one. The generator prunes the pattern; the hand-declared placement finding has advisory severity. A refrain label or a matching string cannot verify who sang a burden. Extended AABA can require a second contrasting B. In historical main, the bridge registry convention is “once”, but a stated count such as `sections.bridge=2` lifts that planner preference.
