# Song Forms and poetic meter

**The explorer can show a documented metrical expectation for a form. Deciding how a particular line scans is a separate operation.** All 364 catalog forms describe types. A performance, an edition of a poem, a pronunciation, and an analyst's reading are occurrences with their own evidence. A meter reference attached to a diagram should say which part it concerns, what unit it measures, which reading it assumes and whether the association is required, typical, optional or disputed.

## The 203-entry crosswalk

[meter-crosswalk.tsv](meter-crosswalk.tsv) covers every original ID from M001 through M203 in order. The names and IDs come from the team's catalog. Its three positionally joined matrices distinguish attestation, proposed representation and operational evidence. The TSV applies the six-row v2 corrections and then v3, including the 32 representability and 13 operations updates. The eight compact evidence fields use the later fixed research snapshot's legend. Rows also link subsequent source discussions where the IDs are mentioned. Those links are provenance. They do not silently alter the snapshot or confer fixture approval. All three matrices and all 203 rows were checked for exact ID alignment. The crosswalk identifies **88 items** with a specific prototype example, counterpart, component, comparison or qualified analogy. **115** retain their original ID without an asserted direct form link. That is a decision about evidence in this comparison. A missing link does not show that a meter has never occurred in English. The original v3 attestation statuses include 60 verified for their specific claim, 137 unverified, and six with individually qualified statuses. “Verified” can mean an English teaching example, a published definition or a particular occurrence. The fixed research snapshot additionally found 94 IDs without a mapped source scan or analysis. Neither number measures functioning recognition. ## Verified current implementation

Source inspection is pinned to main [`d336c17f51`](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/commit/d336c17f51836f03e9a99e7a6a55cbbafa86dbf1) in this review. Runtime claims below name the actual surface. No production requests were sent.

| Surface | What it actually does |
|---|---|
| Harness declared meter | Reads the configured pronunciation stream, records lexical source and aligned prefix, counts syllables, and compares pronounced strong positions to the supplied strong slots. Its basic result explicitly leaves weak slots unchecked. A ten-syllable match is insufficient to identify pentameter. [Reader and baseline comparator](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/blob/d336c17f51836f03e9a99e7a6a55cbbafa86dbf1/lyric-harness/lyric_harness.py#L5332) |
| Strict declared check | Accepts a supplied dot/slash template. It compares every aligned weak and strong position, retains lexical digits 0/1/2, projects 1 and 2 to strong, and names mismatches. Initial inversion is the narrowly scoped declared option. An undecidable suffix cannot earn a full match. The CLI exposes this option. [Strict comparator](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/blob/d336c17f51836f03e9a99e7a6a55cbbafa86dbf1/lyric-harness/quality/meter_strict.py#L80) · [CLI](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/blob/d336c17f51836f03e9a99e7a6a55cbbafa86dbf1/lyric-harness/lyric_harness.py#L11348) · [Existing regressions](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/blob/d336c17f51836f03e9a99e7a6a55cbbafa86dbf1/lyric-harness/quality/test_meter_strict.py#L47) |

An explicit beat grid requires a cycle, evidence basis and positions tied to actual line/unit identities. The cycle model handles grouped musical pulses; its bars are not poetic feet. **Executed here.** I ran the unmodified pinned Python strict comparator on supplied stress vectors. With an iambic five-foot template, `0101010101` held at all ten positions. `1010101010` failed at ten and `2101010101` failed at one weak slot. They did not tokenize a live poem, choose its best lexical reading or exercise the writing connector.

## Optional typed links

Use the two existing IDs already present on either side. The crosswalk's exact title is a lookup key for the prototype; the builder assigns every form a unique checked slug. M001–M203 are review IDs of heterogeneous research subjects. An overlay assembled from the crosswalk can link to them without adding any to the section-function enum or running a second scansion engine.

| Catalog anchor | Kind, scope and strength | Evidence to keep separate |
|---|---|---|
| Heroic couplet → M033 pentameter and M044 iambic family | Per line; defining template in this English form. The two-line parent supplies the pairing. | AA is rhyme. A specific line's fit would require its text, pronunciation reading, status and policy. |
| Spenserian stanza → M059; its closing line → M053 | Required ordered profile across nine lines: eight pentameters followed by one hexameter. The last line matters even when a total is identical. | Rhyme sequence and the published scans of two actual lines are separate evidence, not nine scans. |
| Lune → M152 | Kelly variant only, syllables per line 5–3–5. Keep the Collom variant's 3–5–3 word counts in the form data. | Words, syllables and beats remain distinct. |
| Sapphic stanza → M103 | Adonic component at the end of the ordinary setting; classify quantity and English adaptations separately. | A five-syllable line alone does not establish an Adonic scan. |
| AABA 32-bar | Structural repeat and contrast with four eight-bar sections. No mandatory poetry-meter ID follows. | A particular song could have an independently documented stress template; the catalog title supplies neither that nor its time signature. |
| Refrain in a stanza / chorus in a song | Use an explicit occurrence-level relation to a named section or line role when sourced. | A return of words, a repeated tune and a return of metrical pattern are different predicates. The catalog discussion distinguishes twenty section-kind functions and two line-kind functions; the names alone do not activate a writer gate. [#676 contract](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/issues/676) · [Raw line-role ruling](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/issues/676#issuecomment-6674763042) |

One minimal data contract for future discussion is `form_id + meter_research_id + scope path + relation kind + required/typical/optional/disputed + language/theory + source locator`. An actual reading result also needs its occurrence identifier and engine evidence. Do not attach one to a form type. Multiple sourced alternatives may coexist. A diagram with no supported meter association can show its existing form structure alone. The TSV contains suggestions for review, not executable rules.

## Seven checks against misleading nesting

The two researchers independently reproduced these source-level mappings. Their follow-up records limits for every example. It verifies what the publications show, without turning a derived grouping into a source-authored scan. [Original seven witnesses](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/issues/677#issuecomment-6642294141) · [Independent verification and corrections](https://github.com/WeningerII/CodexMusica/issues/677#issuecomment-6642395821)

| Case | Consequence for this explorer |
|---|---|
| Sijo | Three structural lines can contain six phrases and twelve segments; a source may display three or six lines. Palmer's six-line setting is a permitted transformation, not a second attested printing. |
| Hymn 204 | Eight counted phrases align to six displayed web lyric lines. The manual publishes the phrase vector; the researchers derive the mapping. This gives no note, bar or performed-phrase counts. |
| Spenser | The Wauchope edition describes nine ordered line lengths but provides the two cited explicit scans only at the opening and close. |
| Gawain | One witness groups 14 body lines, one bob, four wheel lines; the body length is example-specific. Sources disagree between one and two stresses for the bob. |
| Gray | Strophe, antistrophe and epode have web-text counts 14/14/20 in the opening triad. The reading text is based on 1768; 1757 is the first publication date. The section counts do not settle line scans. |
| Greek trimeter | The three metra and a two-colon division refer to different boundaries. D'Angour's normalized mnemonic has omitted graphic detail; anceps allows either quantity, not an unknown English stress. |
| Pope | The cited analysis places the caesura *within* the third foot, after syllable five. Scans belong to their quoted wording and analyst. Edition punctuation and actual performance are separate evidence. |

## Handoff boundary

Preserve the existing Stage 4/5 sequence for the writer and section work.
